## Q1. How well do LLMs forecast, and which techniques help (retrieval, base rates, ensembling/aggregation, reasoning, fine-tuning)?

### Takeaway
By mid-2026 the best LLM forecasting *systems* are at or near parity with elite human forecasters on some benchmarks: ForecastBench, Metaculus Spring 2026 (gap not statistically significant), and the Sept 2026 Metaculus Cup, where bots took 1st and 2nd. They still trail on others: market-type questions, individual head-to-head comparisons, and one expert comparison where the gap is large. Most of the improvement comes from the base model, retrieval, and aggregating across samples, models and the crowd. Prompt tricks add little, and "reason like a Bayesian" prompts hurt. Explicit base-rate anchoring helps modestly as a prompt and more clearly when built into the scaffold.

### Cited Findings
**Headline accuracy (chronological)**
- Halawi et al. built a retrieval-augmented system that scored Brier 0.179 on a 914-question test set, against 0.149 for the human crowd. It was the first automated system to come close to crowd level. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563); [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/5a5acfd0876c940d81619c1dc60e7748-Paper-Conference.pdf) · T1 (NeurIPS 2024) · Harness: a single-model pipeline from 2024 lost to the crowd by ~0.03 Brier, so an unaggregated harness verdict should not be assumed to beat a market or crowd.
- Schoenegger et al. (*Science Advances*, 2024): an ensemble of 12 LLMs forecasting 31 binary questions was statistically indistinguishable from a crowd of 925 human forecasters in a 3-month tournament. It also beat a no-information benchmark. — [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adp1528) · T1 · Harness: aggregate across several models rather than trusting one model's number.
- ForecastBench (FRI), October 2025 update: superforecasters scored a difficulty-adjusted Brier of 0.081; the best LLM (GPT-4.5) scored 0.101. Frontier LLMs improve by about 0.016 Brier/year, and LLMs already beat the median public forecaster. Extrapolated parity date: November 2026 (95% CI Jan 2026–Nov 2027); June 2026 for dataset questions and Aug 2026 for market questions. — [FRI Substack, "LLMs Are Closing the Gap"](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human); [ForecastBench paper, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839) · T2 (benchmark operator report) · Harness: yearly model gains of ~0.016 Brier are about the size of human–AI gaps, so record the model version with every prediction. **Conflict:** the same source summary says superforecasters lead by "0.017 Brier points", but 0.101 − 0.081 = 0.020. Check before citing.
- Leaderboard snapshot on Jan 29, 2026: superforecasters #1. xAI Grok 4.20 (Preview) and Cassi's `ensemble_2_crowdadj` tied at #2, ahead of GPT-5, o3 and Claude Sonnet 4.5. — [EA Forum, "AI Forecasting in 2026: What 11 Analyses Say"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Spyz3wESZu2eeqhDj/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say) · T3 (community synthesis) · Harness: ensembles that adjust toward the crowd top the leaderboard, so blending with a market or crowd prior is a serious option.
- FRI report "AI models have likely reached parity with superforecasters on ForecastBench" (a secondary source dates it to July 2026): on the tournament leaderboard several systems are statistically indistinguishable from superforecasters. The top system is from Cassi AI, and models from xAI and Google DeepMind are also indistinguishable. Cassi's pipeline generates sub-questions and search queries, retrieves recent context filtered for relevance and recency, forecasts with an ensemble of models, and then has another LLM analyse the reasoning to produce the final forecast. — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity) · T2 · Harness: the winning design is a template to copy: decompose, retrieve fresh sources, forecast with an ensemble, then aggregate with a meta-reviewer.
- Late May 2026 counter-reading: on ForecastBench's Brier Index, superforecasters scored 70.6% and the best LLMs 67.9%. On market questions the gap was 80.3 vs 75.8. — [Parallect AI Research report (May 2026)](https://parallect.ai/reports/ai-superforecasters-parity-may-2026-fb32ac) · T3 (appears AI-generated) · Harness: parity claims depend on the question type, so track Brier separately for market-like and dataset-like questions.
- Methodological critiques of ForecastBench. (a) The comparison is extrapolated, not head-to-head: superforecasters answered a fixed question set once in mid-2024, AI systems are scored on different questions, and difficulty adjustment bridges the two. (b) The "copier line" critique: market-question scores largely measure edge over the market price. Forecasters close only about a third of the gap on questions the market finds hard, and the spread of forecaster advantage across difficulty bins is about twice the superforecasters' entire margin. — [Copier Line post (Nuño Sempere forum)](https://forum.nunosempere.com/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually) · T3 · Harness: always score against a naive "copy the market/base rate" baseline (Brier skill score), not only raw Brier.
- Metaculus AI Benchmark series, bot team vs Pro forecasters (head-to-head/peer scores; negative means bots worse):
  - Q3 2024: −11.3. Q4 2024: −8.9 (p = 0.079, not significant). — [Metaculus Q4 2024 results](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/35291/q4-ai-benchmarking-results/) · T2
  - Q1 2025: −17.7 (95% CI −28.3 to −7.0), p = 0.001. — [Metaculus Q1 2025 results](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/38673/q1-ai-benchmarking-results/) · T2
  - Q2 2025: 54 bot-makers, 348 questions; Pros significantly better (p = 0.00001). The top 3 bot-makers were hobbyists or students. — [Metaculus Q2 2025 results](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/40456/q2-ai-benchmark-results/) · T2
  - Spring 2026 (FutureEval): bot team −1.25 [−4.87, 2.37] over 99 shared questions, not significant. Pros won 64% of questions, but bot wins were larger. 9 of 10 individual Pros still finished ahead of every individual bot. — [FutureEval Spring Results (Metaculus post, GreaterWrong mirror)](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is) · T2
  - Harness: even the best single bot is still worse than a good human, while the team of bots is at parity. The harness gains more from aggregation than from any one run.
- Metaculus Cup (open human competition), bot placement: metac-gemini-2.5-pro+asknews placed 37th of 551 (Summer 2025) and 23rd of 539 (Fall 2025). — [EA Forum synthesis](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Spyz3wESZu2eeqhDj/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say) · T3. At the Metaculus Cup concluding Sept 5, 2026, AI systems took 1st, 2nd and 5th: bot "laertes" first, Mantic second. Mantic then raised a $25M seed round on Sept 18, 2026. — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/ai-takes-1st-2nd-and-5th-places-at-metaculus-cup/tldr); [Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/18/british-ai-startup-mantic-raises-25m-to-build-superhuman-ai-forecasting-after-metaculus-win/); one outlet headlines that "humans still lead head-to-head": [explainx](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/ai-beats-forecasters-metaculus-cup-coverage-2026) · T3 (news; the primary Metaculus leaderboard was not verified) · Harness: state-of-the-art systems are competitive on ranked leaderboards, but this is news-level evidence. Do not cite it as decisive.
- Lu (2025) tested frontier LLMs on 464 Metaculus questions. They "ostensibly surpass the human crowd" but significantly underperform experts: expert Brier 0.0225 vs o3 at 0.1352. Superforecasters make small, frequent updates at 1% granularity; LLMs update more after reading opposing news. — [arXiv 2507.04562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04562) · T2 · Harness: beating the crowd is not the same as beating experts. The unusually low 0.0225 figure is probably specific to the question subset; check it before citing.

**Which techniques help**
- **Retrieval + fine-tuning (Halawi ablations):** performance drops without the fine-tuned reasoning model, and drops further without both retrieval and fine-tuning. The system beats the crowd when it has enough relevant articles and when the crowd is uncertain (crowd forecast 0.3–0.7: Brier 0.199 vs 0.246). It also beats the crowd at earlier retrieval dates. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563) · T1 · Harness: record how many relevant sources were retrieved per verdict, and trust the harness more where the market or crowd is uncertain.
- **Blending with the crowd:** a weighted average of the system and the crowd beats both, unconditionally. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563) · T1 · Harness: where a market, Metaculus or expert-survey probability exists, blend it into the point probability and record the weights.
- **Aggregation / ensembling:** a 12-model LLM crowd reaches human-crowd accuracy ([Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adp1528), T1). With 15 LLMs on 254 prediction-market questions, *learned* aggregators (MLP, logistic regression) beat every individual model and classical rules. The gains depend on error diversity; correlated errors are a structural limit that self-aggregation cannot overcome ([arXiv 2607.18269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18269); [ACM Trans. Social Computing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3839337), T1/T2). Metaculus Q2 2025 lists aggregating multiple forecasts and manually reviewing bot reasoning on past questions as best practices ([Metaculus Q2 2025](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/40456/q2-ai-benchmark-results/), T2) · Harness: ensemble across *different* model families, not only repeated samples from one model, and log each member's probability so correlation can be measured later.
- **The base model matters most, but scaffolding counts:** Metaculus finds the underlying model is the most important factor. A hobbyist using better prompting, agent tools and scaffolding placed 8th, while the template bot placed 44th. — [EA Forum synthesis](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Spyz3wESZu2eeqhDj/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say) · T3 · Harness: re-baseline Brier whenever the underlying model changes.
- **Prompt engineering / base rates:** 38 prompts across Claude 3.5 Sonnet/Haiku, GPT-4o and Llama 3.1 405B gave mostly negligible gains. Referencing base rates gave slight, inconsistent benefits. "Expert" strategies often backfired, and prompts asking for Bayesian reasoning had strong negative effects. — [arXiv 2506.01578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01578) · T2 · Harness: do not expect a prompt instruction to "use a reference class" or "update like a Bayesian" to fix calibration. Build the base rate into the pipeline instead (next item).
- **Explicit base rate + mechanical updates (scaffold, not prompt):** AuditForecast (Sept 2026) anchors each forecast on a base probability from a quantitative baseline model, then applies situational-factor updates by mechanical aggregation in odds space. It improves accuracy and calibration over agentic baselines and beats more expensive deep-research agents on cost-accuracy. — [arXiv 2609.05905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05905) · T2 (very recent) · Harness: store the base rate, its reference class and each log-odds adjustment in the verdicts file, so post-resolution audits can find which step erred.
- **Fine-tuning / RL on outcomes:** outcome-only online RL (modified GRPO/ReMax) on 110k questions, ensembling 7 predictions, gives a 14B model that matches o1's accuracy (Brier 0.193) and beats it on calibration. — [arXiv 2505.17989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17989) · T2 · Harness: not actionable for an API-based harness, but it shows calibration can be trained; a model trained this way may be a better ensemble member.
- **Reasoning / extended thinking:** on KalshiBench, the reasoning-heavy GPT-5.2-XHigh had the *worst* calibration despite comparable accuracy (details in Q2). — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030) · T2 · Harness: more reasoning effort is not a calibration fix; measure it.
- **Evaluation hygiene:** backtests suffer many forms of temporal leakage (for example, news articles used to build questions leak outcomes), and benchmark performance extrapolates poorly to real-world forecasting. — [Paleka et al., ICLR 2026](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/paleka2026iclr-pitfalls/); [arXiv 2506.00723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723) · T1 · Harness: only prospective, dated predictions count toward the harness's Brier. Never score retrospective "predictions" made after information about the outcome existed.
- **Human + LLM:** in a study with N = 991, access to a frontier LLM assistant improved human forecasting accuracy by 24–28% vs control, and by 41% for the "superforecasting" assistant excluding one outlier item. Even a deliberately noisy, overconfident assistant helped (29%). — [ACM TiiS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3707649); [arXiv 2402.07862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07862) · T1 · Harness: harness output is useful as decision support for a human, even before it is independently superhuman.

### Inferences
- The strongest current recipe for the harness, from the combined evidence: retrieve fresh sources → anchor on an explicit base rate → make explicit log-odds adjustments → forecast with a multi-model ensemble → blend with a market or crowd prior where one exists → aggregate, e.g. trimmed mean or median (a learned aggregator only once resolved history exists).
- "Parity" is benchmark- and question-type-specific. The harness should say "comparable to good human forecasters on some benchmarks", not "superhuman", and should give the benchmark and date.
- Since model gains (~0.016 Brier/yr) are about the size of the human–AI gap, monthly Brier trends in the harness will be confounded by model upgrades unless the model version is logged per prediction.

### Gaps
- ForecastBench's current live leaderboard numbers (as of Sept 2026) and the exact date and figures of FRI's "parity" post could not be read: fetch was blocked and only search summaries were available.
- The Metaculus Summer/Q2–Q3 2026 FutureEval results beyond Spring 2026, and the official Metaculus Cup 2026 leaderboard page, were not verified from primary sources.
- Good Judgment's critique ("What ForecastBench Doesn't Measure (Yet)", https://goodjudgment.com/what-forecastbench-doesnt-measure/) was found but its content could not be read.
- No controlled study was found that isolates "explicit reference class" as a scaffold component, as opposed to a prompt instruction, with Brier deltas on live questions. AuditForecast is the closest.

## Q2. How well calibrated are verbalized probabilities in current models; what biases exist; how to improve; how to score and track with few predictions?

### Takeaway
Verbalized probabilities from frontier models are systematically overconfident on genuinely uncertain future events (KalshiBench ECE 0.12–0.40). Extended reasoning can make this worse. Models also show directional tilts (mostly optimism), acquiescence (a lean toward "yes", with means above 50%), round-number preference and poor updating. Verbalized confidence still beats token log-probs for post-RLHF and post-2025 proprietary models, and it can be improved by distractor normalization (DINCO), multi-answer elicitation, ensembling and recalibration. With tens of predictions per month, Brier differences of ~0.02 are statistically invisible: a few hundred resolved forecasts are needed.

### Cited Findings
- **KalshiBench:** 300 Kalshi questions resolving after the models' training cutoffs. All five frontier models tested (Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B, Kimi-K2) are systematically overconfident, with ECE of 0.120–0.395. GPT-5.2-XHigh, the extended-reasoning variant, shows the worst calibration. Only one model achieves a positive Brier Skill Score, i.e. most do worse than the base rate. — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030) · T2 · Harness: raw model probabilities are likely overconfident, so default to shrinkage toward base rates or 50% until the harness's own calibration history shows otherwise.
- **Verbalized vs token probabilities:** for RLHF models (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude), verbalized confidence is better calibrated than the model's conditional token probabilities. Prompting strategies (e.g., considering multiple answer options before stating confidence) plus temperature scaling cut ECE by more than 50% relative. — [Tian et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/) · T1 · Harness: eliciting verbalized probabilities is the right channel; ask the model to list alternatives before giving a number.
- **Post-2025 models:** for LLM-as-a-judge on top-tier proprietary models, verbalized confidence is now the more robust soft score, and the older advice to prefer log-probs no longer holds. An "overconfidence advisory" in the prompt plus self-debate improves calibration and score spread. — [arXiv 2609.10996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10996) · T2 (Sept 2026) · Harness: the judge can emit verbalized confidence, with a standing warning about overconfidence and a debate step.
- **Overconfidence at all scales:** it is present in LLMs of every size; most calibration gains come from higher accuracy rather than lower overconfidence. — [arXiv 2412.14737](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14737v2) · T2 · Harness: upgrading models will not by itself remove overconfidence.
- **DINCO (distractor-normalized confidence):** the model verbalizes confidence independently on several self-generated alternative claims, and the scores are normalized by the total. This beats the best baselines by ~0.09–0.10 ECE (TriviaQA, SimpleQA), gives less saturated confidences, and DINCO with 10 runs beats self-consistency with 100. — [arXiv 2509.25532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25532); [ICLR 2026](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/wang2026iclr-calibrating/) · T1 · Harness: for each verdict dimension, have the model score competing outcomes/hypotheses separately and normalize. This fits naturally with probability bands.
- **Mechanistic cause:** inflated verbalized confidence is driven by identifiable "Confidence Mover Circuits", and targeted inference-time interventions improve calibration. — [arXiv 2604.01457](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01457) · T2 · Harness: not usable via API; it confirms overconfidence is systematic rather than random noise.
- **Acquiescence and round numbers:** the LLM ensemble showed human-like biases, namely an acquiescence effect (ensemble mean above 50% on balanced questions) and a preference for round numbers. — [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adp1528) · T1 · Harness: phrase some questions in the negative (for example "will X *not* happen") and check that p(X) + p(not X) ≈ 1. Flag runs of 0.5/0.7/0.8 values.
- **Directional (optimism) bias:** OptimismBench elicits P(success) and P(failure) for the same scenario, so the asymmetry gives a signed bias without ground truth. Of 16 models from 8 providers, 14 are optimistic; pessimism appears only in Anthropic's frontier tier. Post-training sets the sign of the bias, with opposite shifts in different model families (11 base-vs-chat pairs). — [arXiv 2607.26981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26981) · T2 (July 2026) · Harness: highly relevant for business and opportunity verdicts. Run inverted-pair prompts per verdict and record the asymmetry; the expected sign depends on which model family is used.
- **Hedging:** LLM forecasters show "seemingly unreasonable hedging", hypothesized to come from safety training. — [EA Forum, "Forecasting With LLMs"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/h5zuCuze3vWNWbjJD/forecasting-with-llms-an-open-and-promising-research) · T3 (hypothesis, not measured) · Harness: this plausibly coexists with overconfidence (hedging on some question types, overconfidence on others). Check both ends of the calibration curve.
- **Updating on new information:** in EvolveCast, LLM updates are often inconsistent or overly conservative, and both verbalized and logit confidences stay far from the human reference. Models treat new information as retrieval context rather than as evidence that shifts the posterior. — [arXiv 2509.23936](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23936v2) · T2. **Conflict:** Lu (2025) reports LLMs update *more* when they read opposing news ([arXiv 2507.04562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04562), T2) · Harness: when re-running a verdict after new evidence, log the prior, the evidence and the posterior, so update size can be audited rather than assumed.
- **Extremizing aggregates:** extremizing usually improves aggregate forecasts that are under-confident, but when the pooled prior is biased it can push forecasts further from the truth and increase miscalibration. — [Int. J. Forecasting (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207024000992) · T1 · Harness: do not extremize the ensemble by default, given that the members are already overconfident. Decide only after enough resolved data exists.
- **Scoring with small samples:** the Brier score has an exact, analytically derived sampling variance, and confidence intervals can be computed from moments of the forecasts and outcomes. For infrequent events, *a few hundred* forecast–observation pairs are needed to establish skill. — [Bradley, Schwartz & Hashino 2008, *Weather and Forecasting*](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/23/5/2007waf2007049_1.xml) · T1 · Harness: report every monthly Brier with a confidence interval and a Brier skill score against a base-rate baseline, and claim nothing about skill until N is in the hundreds.
- **Decomposition:** Brier = reliability (calibration) − resolution + uncertainty. Estimators for the variance of these components exist, and the components are noisy at small N. — [arXiv 1303.6182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.6182) · T2 · Harness: publish the reliability/resolution split only on cumulative data, not monthly slices.
- **ICD 203 likelihood lexicon:** almost no chance 1–5%, very unlikely 5–20%, unlikely 20–45%, roughly even chance 45–55%, likely 55–80%, very likely 80–95%, almost certain 95–99%. — [ICD 203 mirror (GitHub, links the official PDF at fas.org)](https://github.com/wesinator/ICD203-intel-analysis) · T1 content via T3 mirror · Harness: map the harness's probability bands one-to-one onto this lexicon so verbal and numeric outputs cannot drift apart. Research shows verbal probabilities are interpreted inconsistently: [Wintle et al., PLOS One 2019, "Verbal probabilities: Very likely to be somewhat more confusing than numbers"](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0213522) (T1, title-level only). Always store the number next to the word.

### Inferences
- Worked example (my own calculation): a forecast of 0.7 on events that occur 70% of the time has a per-item squared-error SD of ≈0.18. The standard error of the mean Brier is therefore ≈0.033 at n = 30, ≈0.018 at n = 100 and ≈0.011 at n = 300. The ~0.02 superforecaster–LLM gap is invisible below a few hundred resolved forecasts, so a monthly Brier from 20–50 predictions is mostly noise. Use a cumulative rolling Brier with bootstrap or analytic CIs, and paired comparisons against a baseline (base rate, market or 0.5) on the *same* questions, which reduces variance.
- For calibration curves at small N, use 3–5 coarse bins aligned to the harness's probability bands (or the ICD 203 bands). Report the observed frequency per bin with Wilson intervals, and do not fit curves until each bin has ~20+ resolved items.
- Default post-processing, pending the harness's own data: shrink ensemble means toward the base rate (for example, fit a Platt or logistic recalibration once ≥100 are resolved), run inverted-pair checks for directional bias, and use DINCO-style normalization across competing outcomes.
- The harness's predictions should be designed to resolve within weeks to months, and in volume. Otherwise the monthly Brier process cannot produce a usable signal within a year.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study was found that measures calibration on multi-dimensional business or strategy verdicts, as opposed to binary event questions. All evidence is on QA or event forecasting.
- The details of the "overconfidence advisory" and self-debate method in arXiv 2609.10996, and any effect sizes, were not retrievable.
- No calibration numbers were found for the newest Claude/GPT/Gemini releases on live forecasting beyond KalshiBench's five models.

## Q3. How often do LLM research/search agents fabricate or misattribute citations, which mitigations have evidence, and how should citation accuracy be evaluated?

### Takeaway
Fabricated URLs are now fairly rare in retrieval-based agents (roughly 3–13% hallucinated, 5–18% non-resolving), and mechanical link checking pushes this below 1%. The dominant failure is *unsupported or misattributed content*: links work (>94%) and look relevant (>80%), but only 39–77% of cited claims are actually supported by the source. Audits of deep-research modes find large fractions of unsupported statements. Closed-book (no retrieval) citation generation remains very bad (14–95% hallucinated). Retrieval is the biggest lever. After that, verification that re-reads the cited source is required, because LLM judges without retrieval miss most bad citations.

### Cited Findings
**Measured rates**
- **Tow Center (CJR, March 2025):** 8 AI search engines (ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Perplexity Pro, Gemini, DeepSeek Search, Grok-2, Grok-3, Copilot), 1,600 queries (200 each) asking for the source article of a quote. Engines got it wrong more than 60% of the time. More than half of Gemini's and Grok 3's citations led to fabricated or broken URLs (Grok 3: 154 of 200). They rarely declined to answer, premium tiers were more *confidently* wrong, and they often cited syndicated copies. — [CJR / Tow Center](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php); [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/03/ai-search-engines-fail-to-produce-accurate-citations-in-over-60-of-tests-according-to-new-tow-center-study/) · T2 (journalism research) · Harness: consumer AI search is not a trustworthy citation source. The harness must fetch and check the original publisher URL itself.
- **EBU/BBC "News Integrity in AI Assistants" (Oct 2025):** 22 public-service media organisations in 18 countries and 14 languages, 3,000+ responses from ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity rated by journalists. 45% had at least one significant issue; 31% had serious sourcing problems (missing or misleading attribution); 20% had major accuracy issues. Gemini had significant issues in 76% of responses. — [EBU report](https://www.ebu.ch/research/open/report/news-integrity-in-ai-assistants) · T2 · Harness: roughly one in three answers has sourcing problems, so every claim needs source-level verification, not trust in the assistant's attribution.
- **DeepTRACE (ICLR 2026):** audited GPT-4.5/5, You.com, Perplexity, Copilot/Bing and Gemini on 8 dimensions, using statement-level decomposition, citation and factual-support matrices, and an LLM judge validated against humans. Citation accuracy ranged from 40% to 80% across systems. Deep-research configurations reduced overconfidence and raised citation thoroughness, but still had large fractions of unsupported statements (74–97% in some configurations, per the search summary) and were highly one-sided on debate queries. — [arXiv 2509.04499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499); [ICLR 2026](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/venkit2026iclr-deeptrace/) · T1 · Harness: "deep research" mode does not guarantee support. The 74–97% range should be re-checked in the full text before use as a decisive figure.
- **"Cited but Not Verified" (May 2026):** 14 LLMs including GPT-5.2, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet/Opus/Haiku, Gemini 3.1 Pro and 3 Flash, and open models, on 130 DeepResearch Bench and BrowseComp queries. It parses inline Markdown citations with an AST parser, retrieves the cited page and scores Link Works, Relevant Content and Fact Check. Even frontier models keep link validity above 94% and relevance above 80%, yet reach only **39–77% factual accuracy** against the cited source. — [arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635) · T2 · Harness: the three-level check (link resolves → content relevant → claim supported) maps directly onto the harness's citation fields. Only the third level catches most errors.
- **Reference hallucinations in commercial LLMs and deep-research agents (Apr 2026):** 10 models and agents on DRBench (53,090 URLs) plus 3 models on ExpertQA (168,021 URLs, 32 fields). 3–13% of URLs were hallucinated and 5–18% did not resolve. Deep-research agents had the highest hallucination rates despite producing more citations, and retrieval architecture mattered more than output volume. Some models fabricate every non-resolving URL; others show stale-but-real links. — [arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173) · T2 · Harness: expect roughly 1 in 10–20 URLs to be dead or fake before checking.
- **ReportBench (academic survey tasks):** OpenAI Deep Research's reference precision was 0.385 vs Gemini's 0.145. Gemini produces 3× more cited statements (32.42 vs 9.89) with no gain in recall. Citation match rate was 78.87% vs 72.94%; factual accuracy of uncited statements was 95.83% vs 92.21%. Failure modes: non-existent findings attributed to real papers, or fabricated URLs/DOIs. — [arXiv 2508.15804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15804) · T2 · Harness: more citations does not mean better support. Grade citation quality, not count.
- **SourceCheckup (medicine; *Nature Communications* 2025):** 7 LLMs, 800 questions, 58,000 statement–source pairs. 50–90% of responses are not fully supported by, and are sometimes contradicted by, their cited sources. For GPT-4o with web search, ~30% of statements are unsupported and nearly half of responses are not fully supported. Physicians validated the automated verifier. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58551-6) · T1 · Harness: an automated statement-to-source verifier can be validated against human experts, which is a model for the harness's judge.
- **Closed-book citation generation (no retrieval):** GhostCite tested 13 LLMs across 40 CS domains (375,440 citations) and found hallucination rates of 14.23% (DeepSeek) to 94.93% (Hunyuan). Across 56,381 published AI/ML and security papers, 1.07% contain invalid citations, up 80.9% in 2025. Its survey of 97 researchers found 87% claim to always verify AI citations, but 42% paste BibTeX unchecked and 77% of reviewers do not check references thoroughly (survey figures via search summary). — [arXiv 2602.06718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06718) · T2. GPT-4o fabricated 19.9% of citations across six simulated mental-health literature reviews (JMIR Mental Health). — [EurekAlert press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1106130) · T2 (press release of a peer-reviewed study) · Harness: never let a model supply a citation from memory. Citations must come from retrieved documents in the run.
- **Error propagation in multi-agent deep research:** deep-research systems have poor citation recall. Content and citations get corrupted as they pass between agents "like a telephone game". Evaluating each agent invocation against its *own inputs* pinpoints which agent introduced an error. — [arXiv 2608.24306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24306) · T2 (Aug 2026) · Harness: log each subagent's inputs and outputs so a failed citation can be traced to the retriever, the summarizer or the writer.

**Mitigations with evidence**
- **Retrieval first:** moving from zero-shot to RAG is the biggest driver of attribution quality. On FEVER, correctness for generation-time citation rose from ~27% to ~77%; on LongBench-Cite, coverage rose from ~11% to ~58%. There is a stable trade-off: post-hoc citation (P-Cite) gives high coverage with competitive correctness, while generation-time citation (G-Cite) favours precision over coverage. The authors recommend a retrieval-centric, P-Cite-first design for high-stakes use and G-Cite for strict claim verification. — [arXiv 2509.21557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21557); [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/122374) · T1 · Harness: for the 2–3 decisive claims, use the precision-first mode (cite while generating, from retrieved text).
- **Mechanical URL health check:** the urlhealth tool cut non-resolving URLs to below 1% for all three models tested, but its effectiveness depends on the model's tool-use competence. — [arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173) · T2 · Harness: run a deterministic HTTP and archive check on *every* citation before the judge sees it. This is cheap and nearly eliminates dead or fake links.
- **Quote-grounded citation (span extraction):** Anthropic's Citations API improved recall accuracy by up to 15% over custom implementations, per internal evals. Customer Endex reported source hallucinations and formatting issues falling from 10% to 0%. Caveat: this guarantees the cited span exists in the source, not that the model's *interpretation* of it is correct. — [Anthropic announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-citations-api) · T2 (vendor claim) · Harness: store the verbatim quote alongside each claim, which makes judge verification a comparison of claim against quote. A separate entailment check is still needed.
- **Tool-augmented verification vs a bare LLM judge:** CiteGuard reaches 68% on CiteME, close to human performance (70%). A plain LLM-as-judge without tool-augmented retrieval achieves only 16–17% recall. — [arXiv 2510.17853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17853) · T2 · Harness: the judge must fetch and read the cited page. A judge reasoning from the claim alone will miss most bad citations.
- **Automatic attribution evaluation is hard:** on AttributionBench, even fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reaches only ~80% macro-F1 on "is this claim supported by the cited evidence?". Errors come from insensitivity to fine-grained details (numbers, qualifiers) and from the model seeing only extracted evidence while humans see the whole page. — [AttributionBench, ACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.886.pdf) · T1 · Harness: the judge should check exact numbers, dates and qualifiers, and should read the full page context rather than only the snippet.

**How to evaluate citation accuracy (consolidated from the above)**
- Use a three-tier scheme: (1) the link resolves and points to the claimed publisher, not a syndicated copy; (2) the content is relevant; (3) the claim is entailed by the source, with the verbatim span. Support the third tier with statement-level decomposition and a support matrix (DeepTRACE, SourceCheckup), plus per-agent localization (arXiv 2608.24306). — sources as cited above.

### Inferences
- With unsupported-claim rates of 23–61% in the best systems ("Cited but Not Verified", factual accuracy 39–77%), a judge that checks only the 2–3 most decisive citations will catch the errors that matter most for the verdict but leave many others. Pair it with automated link checks on all citations, plus an automated quote-entailment check on every claim that carries a number.
- Precise statistics, meaning numbers, dates and percentages, are the most common kind of misattribution in audits. Require a verbatim quote for every numeric claim, and have the judge prioritise numeric decisive claims.
- The harness's "tier" field should distinguish the tier of the source (primary vs secondary) from the verification status (link OK, quote matched, entailment confirmed, verified date). They are different kinds of information.

### Gaps
- The search summary also mentioned CiteAudit (Yuan et al., 2026: 6,475 real + 2,967 fake citations; a multi-agent verifier with 97% accuracy on generated cases and 90% on real-world cases) and SemanticCite (Haan, 2025: 84% weighted accuracy across supported/partial/unsupported/uncertain). Primary URLs were not retrieved, so these are unverified leads.
- One summary mentions a failure mode in which "the orchestrator attaches a fabricated precise statistic to a source that does not state it", but it could not be tied to a specific paper.
- No Tow Center or EBU follow-up from 2026 was verified (search budget exhausted). It is unknown whether the 2025 error rates improved in 2026 products.
- The full DeepTRACE per-system tables could not be read, so the "74–97% unsupported" figure is from a summary only.

## Q4. Is there evidence on structured analytic techniques (ICD 203, ACH, key assumptions check, pre-mortem) applied with or by LLMs?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin. Evidence on humans suggests ACH as a whole gives little or no accuracy benefit. The only LLM-on-tradecraft evaluation found (*IJIC*, 2026) concludes that LLMs are useful for brainstorming and outlining, not for all-source judgment. The closest effective analogues are LLM devil's advocates (randomized evidence of better group decisions) and structured base-rate-plus-adjustment forecasting scaffolds. LLMs also show confirmation bias in how they select evidence and a 20–30% upward bias in evidence–hypothesis likelihoods, which is exactly what ACH-style scoring depends on.

### Cited Findings
- **ACH in humans:** an experiment with intelligence analysts found ACH as a whole has little to no benefit on judgment quality and may harm it. ACH-trained analysts did not follow all the steps, evidence on reducing confirmation bias was mixed, and ACH may increase inconsistency and error. — [Dhami et al., *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 2019](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/69049/1/Dhami_etal_ACP_2019_The_analysis_of_competing_hypotheses_in_intelligence.pdf) · T1. A 2024 critical review in *Intelligence and National Security* reviews the ACH technique and draws lessons for the intelligence community. — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02684527.2024.2304934) · T1 (title/abstract only) · Harness: do not assume that running ACH makes verdicts more accurate. Treat it as documentation and a check on bias, and measure its effect on Brier.
- **LLMs evaluated against tradecraft standards:** LLM outputs were compared with human analysts against analytic tradecraft standards. True all-source analysis by AI is "likely several generations of improvement away"; current good uses are brainstorming, wordsmithing and editing, and outlining approaches. — [*International Journal of Intelligence and CounterIntelligence* 39(3), 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08850607.2026.2618633) · T1 (abstract only) · Harness: use SATs to structure and audit the harness's own reasoning. Do not treat an LLM's ICD 203-style prose as evidence of analytic quality.
- **LLM likelihoods for evidence–hypothesis pairs (the core of ACH and Bayesian process tracing):** on 289 evidence–hypothesis pairs, GPT likelihood estimates were on average 30–40% away from expert estimates, mostly an upward bias of 20–30%, which changed the posterior ranking of competing hypotheses. — [*Chinese Political Science Review* 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41111-026-00335-4) · T1 · Harness: an LLM-filled ACH matrix or likelihood table can rank hypotheses in the wrong order. Calibrate or sanity-check the likelihoods, and prefer relative ("more consistent with H1 than H2") over absolute judgments.
- **Confirmation bias in LLM hypothesis testing:** models show confirmation bias in *which* evidence they choose to test, with task success from 6% to 78% within 45 turns. — [arXiv 2604.02485](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02485v1) · T2 · Harness: the harness's evidence collection should be driven by explicit disconfirming queries for each hypothesis, i.e. the "diagnostic evidence" step of ACH, not left to the model.
- **LLM devil's advocate (randomized experiment):** in AI-assisted group decisions, LLM devil's advocates that argue *against the AI's recommendation* can improve decisions without substantially raising workload. Interactive versions were perceived as higher quality. — [Chiang et al., IUI 2024 (ACM)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3640543.3645199) · T1 · Harness: supports the advocate/skeptic subagent design, provided the skeptic targets the leading verdict rather than making generic objections.
- **Pre-mortem / "could you be wrong?":** a metacognitive-prompt paper reports that prior human research found asking people to consider how their initial judgment might be wrong before re-estimating improved results by roughly an order of magnitude compared with simply making a second judgment. The paper proposes applying this prompt to LLM-assisted decisions. — [arXiv 2507.10124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10124) · T2 (reported secondhand; the underlying human study was not verified) · Harness: a pre-mortem step ("assume this verdict turned out wrong; why?") followed by re-estimation is cheap and has plausible support. Measure it.
- **Structured base rate + adjustments in forecasting** (see Q1): AuditForecast's explicit base probability plus odds-space factor updates improved accuracy and calibration. — [arXiv 2609.05905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05905) · T2 · Harness: this is the best-evidenced "structured technique" for LLM probability output and maps onto a key-assumptions check (each adjustment is a named assumption).
- **ICD 203 probability language:** the standard ties verbal likelihood terms to numeric ranges (see Q2) and sets analytic standards used in training and product evaluation, including a dedicated uncertainty standard. — [ICD 203 mirror](https://github.com/wesinator/ICD203-intel-analysis); [Pangea Research on Tradecraft Standard 2: Uncertainty](https://pangearesearch.substack.com/p/analytic-tradecraft-standard-2-uncertainty) · T1 via T3 mirror / T3 · Harness: adopt the ICD 203 lexicon for the verbal bands and its uncertainty standard (state confidence and the basis for it separately from likelihood). This matches the harness's two-axis confidence design.

### Inferences
- The SAT components with the best support for the harness are: (1) explicit alternatives or competing outcomes scored separately (DINCO in Q2; ACH's matrix only if likelihoods are sanity-checked); (2) base rate plus named adjustments, which works as a key assumptions check; (3) a targeted devil's advocate or pre-mortem; (4) the ICD 203 numeric–verbal lexicon. Full ACH as a ritual has weak evidence even in humans.
- Because LLM likelihood judgments are biased upward, ACH matrices filled by LLMs should use inconsistency counting (Heuer's original emphasis on disconfirming evidence) rather than multiplying LLM-estimated likelihoods.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found of LLMs *performing* ACH, a key assumptions check or a pre-mortem with measured accuracy or Brier outcomes. The *IJIC* 2026 study's detailed results were behind a paywall and only the abstract was seen.
- No evidence was found on LLM compliance with the full set of ICD 203 tradecraft standards scored by rubric.
- Practitioner write-ups exist (for example, https://sroberts.io/posts/llm-sats-ftw) but were not read and contain no measured outcomes, per the search summary.
