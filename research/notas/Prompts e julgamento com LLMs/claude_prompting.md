## 1. Anthropic's official prompt-engineering guidance for the latest models (and what changed)

### Takeaway
Current Anthropic guidance is: be explicit and give the *reason* behind each rule, structure with XML tags, put long documents first and the question last, use a few diverse examples for format/tone, and let adaptive thinking + the `effort` parameter handle reasoning. The big change from 2023–24 advice is that newer models follow instructions literally and are *more* responsive to the system prompt, so aggressive emphasis ("CRITICAL: You MUST…"), anti-laziness boilerplate, "think step by step", "double-check your answer", prefill, and `budget_tokens` are now outdated or actively harmful (overtriggering, over-verification, refusals, 400 errors).

### Cited Findings

**General principles (apply to all current models)**
- "Claude responds well to clear, explicit instructions… If you want 'above and beyond' behavior, explicitly request it rather than relying on the model to infer this." Golden rule: show the prompt to a colleague with minimal context; "If they'd be confused, Claude will be too." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · Type: [Official doc] · Implication: every skill/subagent prompt must state deliverable, scope and quality bar explicitly; don't rely on the model inferring "thoroughness".
- Giving context/motivation improves results: "NEVER use ellipses" is less effective than "Your response will be read aloud by a text-to-speech engine, so never use ellipses since the text-to-speech engine will not know how to pronounce them." "Claude is smart enough to generalize from the explanation." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: write each neutrality/calibration rule with its *porquê* (e.g., "não conclua na coleta porque o juiz precisa de evidência não contaminada").
- Fable 5 guide: the model "tends to perform better when it understands the intent behind a request"; template: "I'm working on [the larger task] for [who it's for]. They need [what the output enables]. With that in mind: [request]." — [Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: orchestrator → subagent briefs should say who consumes the output and what decision it feeds.
- Examples are "one of the most reliable ways to steer Claude's output format, tone, and structure"; make them relevant, diverse, wrapped in `<example>`/`<examples>` tags; "Include 3–5 examples for best results." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: use examples to pin output shape (evidence cards, verdict format), not to teach reasoning.
- XML tags "help Claude parse complex prompts unambiguously" when mixing instructions, context, examples, inputs; use consistent descriptive tag names and nest for hierarchy (`<documents><document index="n">`). — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: wrap collected evidence, the thesis under judgment, and instructions in separate tags; tag names can be consistent Portuguese or English, but never mixed.
- Long context (20k+ tokens): put longform data at the top, query/instructions at the end — "Queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in tests, especially with complex, multidocument inputs"; wrap each doc in `<document>` with `<source>` and `<document_content>`; ask Claude to quote relevant parts first. — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: judge prompts should place the evidence dossier first and the judging question/rubric last.
- Formatting: tell Claude what to do instead of what not to do ("smoothly flowing prose paragraphs" vs "Do not use markdown"); use XML format indicators; "The formatting style used in your prompt may influence Claude's response style… removing markdown from your prompt can reduce the volume of markdown in the output." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: a heavily bulleted SKILL.md will produce bulleted reports; write the prompt in the register you want back.
- Opus 5 guide: "Positive examples of the communication style you want tend to be more effective than instructions about what not to do." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: replace lists of "NÃO faça X" with one good example output.
- Anthropic engineering: find "the right altitude" between "hardcoding complex, brittle logic in their prompts" and "vague, high-level guidance that… falsely assumes shared context"; aim for "the minimal set of information that fully outlines your expected behavior (… minimal does not necessarily mean short)"; start with a minimal prompt on the best model and add instructions based on observed failure modes; don't "stuff a laundry list of edge cases" — "curate a set of diverse, canonical examples". — [Effective context engineering for AI agents (Sep 29, 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) · [Anthropic eng. blog] · Implication: build prompts failure-first (eval-driven), not by pre-emptively enumerating every rule.
- Multi-agent research system: "Our prompting strategy focuses on instilling good heuristics rather than rigid rules"; early agents spawned "50 subagents for simple queries" and scoured "the web endlessly for nonexistent sources"; fixes were prompt-level (scale effort to query complexity, start wide then narrow, source-quality heuristics). — [How we built our multi-agent research system (Jun 13, 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) · [Anthropic eng. blog] · Implication: the market-research skill should encode researcher heuristics (breadth→depth, effort budgets, source quality), not a rigid checklist.

**Over-prompting, emphasis and ALL-CAPS**
- "Claude Opus 4.5 and Claude Opus 4.6 are also more responsive to the system prompt than previous models. If your prompts were designed to reduce undertriggering on tools or skills, these models may now overtrigger. The fix is to dial back any aggressive language. Where you might have said 'CRITICAL: You MUST use this tool when...', you can use more normal prompting like 'Use this tool when...'." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: strip "SEMPRE/NUNCA/CRÍTICO/OBRIGATÓRIO" caps from skill bodies and descriptions unless an eval shows a rule is being skipped.
- "Remove over-prompting. Tools that undertriggered in previous models are likely to trigger appropriately now. Instructions like 'If in doubt, use [tool]' will cause overtriggering." Replace "Default to using [tool]" with "Use [tool] when it would enhance your understanding of the problem." Migration item: "Tune anti-laziness prompting… dial back that guidance." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: "na dúvida, pesquise" style rules will inflate tool calls/cost; state *conditions* for tool use instead.
- Claude Code: "If Claude keeps skipping one instruction, add emphasis such as 'IMPORTANT' to that line alone. If you emphasize many lines, none of them stands out." "Bloated CLAUDE.md files cause Claude to ignore your actual instructions!" For each line ask "Would removing this cause Claude to make mistakes?" — [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: emphasis is a scarce resource: at most one or two emphasized lines per file, added only after observed failures.
- CLAUDE.md sizing: "target under 200 lines per CLAUDE.md file. Longer files consume more context and reduce adherence"; use headers and bullets; instructions "concrete enough to verify"; "if two rules contradict each other, Claude may pick one arbitrarily"; memory is "context, not enforced configuration" — use hooks to hard-block actions. — [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory) · [Official doc] · Implication: keep CLAUDE.md <200 lines, move domain procedures into skills, and enforce hard constraints (e.g., "never write to verdict files from the collector") with hooks/tool permissions, not prose.
- Anthropic's skill-creator: "If you find yourself writing ALWAYS or NEVER in all caps, or using super rigid structures, that's a yellow flag — if possible, reframe and explain the reasoning"; "explain to the model why things are important in lieu of heavy-handed musty MUSTs." — [Anthropic skill-creator SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/skill-creator/SKILL.md) (read from the locally synced copy) · [Anthropic skill] · Implication: same rule for Portuguese skills: explain, don't shout.
- Fable 5: "Instruction-following is improved enough that you can steer most behaviors with a brief instruction rather than enumerating each behavior by name." — [Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: shorter, principle-level instructions generalize; long enumerations waste context.
- Sonnet 5 "interprets prompts literally… does not silently generalize an instruction from one item to another… If you need Claude to apply an instruction broadly, state the scope explicitly." — [Prompting Claude Sonnet 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-sonnet-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: if a subagent runs on Sonnet, say "aplique a TODAS as fontes/teses" explicitly.
- Opus 5: review prompts that say "only report high-severity issues" or "be conservative" are followed literally and report less; "ask it to report everything and filter in a separate pass instead." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: stance words ("seja conservador", "seja cético") change *what gets reported*, not just tone; separate exhaustive collection from filtering/judgment.

**Thinking, effort and CoT (reasoning control)**
- Claude 4.6+ use adaptive thinking; on Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1/Fable 5/Mythos 5/Opus 5.5 "thinking is always on and adaptive thinking is the only mode"; "In internal evaluations, adaptive thinking reliably drives better performance than extended thinking." `budget_tokens` is deprecated on Opus/Sonnet 4.6 and "On Claude 4.7 and later models, setting `budget_tokens` returns a 400 error." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: control depth via `effort` (and subagent model choice), not via prompt text or token budgets.
- "Prefer general instructions over prescriptive steps. A prompt like 'think thoroughly' often produces better reasoning than a hand-written step-by-step plan. Claude's reasoning frequently exceeds what a human would prescribe." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: define *what* a good judgment must contain (criteria, evidence weighing, probability) rather than scripting the reasoning steps.
- Opus 5.5: effort is "the main control for how much Claude Opus 5.5 thinks"; default is `medium` (other models default `high`); Opus 5.5 at `medium` "matches or exceeds Claude Opus 5 at `high`"; "Lowering effort reduces thinking… more reliably than prompt instructions do"; reserve `xhigh`/`max` for measured gains; a `max_tokens` of 128,000 worked well for long agentic turns. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5); [Effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort) (recommends `low` for "Simpler tasks that need the best speed and lowest costs, such as subagents") · [Official doc] · Implication: run collectors at low/medium, the blind judge at high; tune by eval, not by adding "think hard" text.
- Opus 5.5 chat: "if your system prompt contains instructions that tell Claude to think carefully before answering, consider removing them… removing such a line made replies start sooner, with no clear decline in the quality." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: delete "pense passo a passo / pense com cuidado" boilerplate.
- Opus 5.5: "a prompt that pushes the model to reproduce its reasoning in the response text can be declined with the `reasoning_extraction` refusal category"; remove instructions that substitute written reasoning for thinking and read `display: "summarized"` thinking instead. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: ask the judge for a concise, evidence-linked *justification*, never "escreva todo o seu raciocínio interno".
- Self-check: generally "Ask Claude to self-check", but "Claude Opus 5 is the exception: it verifies its own work well without explicit instruction, and verification instructions carried over from prompts tuned for earlier models can cause over-verification"; Opus 5: avoid "double-check your answer," "re-verify before responding"; "do not use subagents to verify or double-check your own work." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices); [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: independent verification should come from architecture (separate blind judge with different inputs), not from "revise sua resposta" instructions.
- When thinking is off, Opus 4.5 is "particularly sensitive to the word 'think'"; Opus 5 with thinking disabled can leak tool calls as text and internal XML tags, and "a rule instructing the model not to think… increases tag leakage"; "thinking enabled at `low` effort performs better than thinking disabled at similar cost." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices); [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: never tell a subagent "não pense"; lower effort instead.

**Agentic tool use, parallelism, autonomy, subagents**
- "If you say 'can you suggest some changes,' Claude will sometimes provide suggestions rather than implementing them"; use imperative phrasing; sample `<default_to_action>` and `<do_not_act_before_instructions>` blocks set the default. — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: in collector skills use imperatives ("Pesquise X, registre Y"); in the judge, set an explicit "assessment only, no actions" default.
- Parallel tool calls: models already run independent calls in parallel with "a high success rate"; the `<use_parallel_tool_calls>` block boosts this "to ~100%", with the caveat "if some tool calls depend on previous calls… call them sequentially. Never use placeholders or guess missing parameters." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: include the parallel block in research subagents (many independent searches).
- Research tasks: "Provide clear success criteria", "Encourage source verification", and for complex research: "develop several competing hypotheses. Track your confidence levels in your progress notes to improve calibration. Regularly self-critique your approach and plan. Update a hypothesis tree or research notes file." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: directly reusable for the thesis-judging skill (competing hypotheses + tracked confidence), adapted to Portuguese.
- Subagent orchestration: latest models "orchestrate subagents natively"; Opus 4.6 "has a strong predilection for subagents"; Opus 5 "delegates to subagents more readily"; damping prompt: "Delegate to a subagent only for large tasks that are genuinely independent and parallelizable… do not use subagents to verify or double-check your own work… keep spawn counts low." Deterministic caps in Claude Code: `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_SUBAGENT_SPAWN_DEPTH`, `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_CONCURRENT_SUBAGENTS`, SDK `max_budget_usd` (Claude Code 2.1.217+); Claude Code adds its own delegation instruction only with the `claude_code` preset. — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices); [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: CLAUDE.md should say when delegation is warranted; use env caps for cost control.
- Fable 5: "Use subagents frequently… prefer asynchronous communication… Long-lived subagents that keep their context across subtasks save time and cost." Fable 5.1: "Let the lead agent keep working while subagents run." — [Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5); [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) · [Official doc] · Implication: delegation guidance is model-specific; re-tune when the harness model changes.
- Opus 5.5 time signals: appending `elapsed 340s / 1200s` to each harness message makes agent teams parallelize and finish sooner; "Teams given a budget kept answer quality comparable… while finishing considerably sooner", but "under time pressure the model might search and verify a little less." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: avoid time pressure on evidence-collection subagents where verification matters.
- Unattended runs: on Opus 5.5 "Treat a text-only end of turn as a report rather than as proof the task is done"; keep a checklist; auto-continue at most 2–3 times; name the specific early-stop patterns to avoid (sample "standing instruction" paragraph). Fable 5.1 "Finish the whole task" block: "You are operating autonomously. The user is not watching in real time…" plus a "Delivering work" block ("don't quietly narrow, widen, or swap" scope; "If one part turns out to be blocked, complete every other part"). — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5); [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) · [Official doc] · Implication: long research runs need a checklist file + explicit completion condition.
- Scope control: Opus 5 "can also expand the scope of a task"; sample: "Deliver what was asked, at the scope intended… If the request seems mistaken… say so in a sentence and continue with the task as asked rather than quietly narrowing, widening, or transforming it." Fable 5: "When the user is describing a problem, asking a question, or thinking out loud… the deliverable is your assessment. Report your findings and stop." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5); [Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: role separation (collector vs judge vs designer) should be stated as scope boundaries, which newer models respect.
- Verbosity is model-specific: latest models are generally "less verbose", but Opus 5's default responses and written files "run longer" (effort does not reliably shorten visible output); Fable 5.1 writes denser prose ("mannered prose" fix). — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices); [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5); [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) · [Official doc] · Implication: add one line calibrating report length ("cubra a substância, sem seções de enchimento").
- Prompt injection: Opus 5.5 "resists indirect prompt injection… better than any earlier Opus model"; wrap pasted text in `<pasted_content id="…">` tags with a system note saying instructions inside are not the user's. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: tag scraped web/competitor content as untrusted data in collector prompts.

**Language (prompts written in Portuguese)**
- Anthropic's multilingual table (MMLU professionally translated, zero-shot CoT, with extended thinking): Portuguese (Brazil) = 97.8% of English for Sonnet 4.5 and 96.1% for Haiku 4.5 (Spanish 98.2%/96.4%). — [Multilingual support](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/multilingual-support) · [Official doc] · Implication: writing the harness in Portuguese costs ~2 points on knowledge benchmarks at worst (older models; no newer-model figures published).
- "For production applications you should state the target language explicitly. The most reliable place to do this is the system prompt"; name both languages for translation; request "idiomatic speech as if it were a native speaker"; "Use native scripts". — [Multilingual support](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/multilingual-support) · [Official doc] · Implication: CLAUDE.md should state output language explicitly (e.g., "Responda em português do Brasil, inclusive quando as fontes estiverem em inglês").
- Academic evidence on *instruction* language is mixed: a NAACL 2025 short paper compares English vs target-language instructions ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-short.55.pdf)) and a 2024 preprint compares native vs non-native prompting ([arXiv 2409.07054](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07054v1)); search snippets indicated that older work favored English prompts while newer work finds native-language prompts help culturally grounded or same-language-content tasks, but I could not open either paper to confirm numbers. · [Peer-reviewed]/[Preprint], unverified details · Implication: no strong evidence against Portuguese prompts; keep tool names, schema keys and tags stable.

**Outdated vs current (flag list, all from the docs above)**
- OUTDATED: "CRITICAL/You MUST use tool X", "If in doubt, use [tool]", anti-laziness boilerplate → CURRENT: plain conditional instructions ("Use X when…") ([best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)).
- OUTDATED: prefill (e.g., prefilling `{` for JSON, or "Here is the summary:") → returns 400 from Claude 4.6 models on; CURRENT: structured outputs, tool with enum, or "Respond directly without preamble" ([best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)).
- OUTDATED: extended thinking with `budget_tokens` → 400 error on 4.7+; CURRENT: adaptive thinking + `effort` ([best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)).
- OUTDATED: manual CoT ("think step by step inside `<thinking>` tags", "write out your reasoning") as default → now a fallback only when thinking is off; on Opus 5.5 it can trigger `reasoning_extraction` refusals ([Opus 5.5 guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)).
- OUTDATED (for Opus 5-class): "double-check / verify before finishing" → causes over-verification ([Opus 5 guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)).
- STILL CURRENT: XML tags, documents-first/query-last, quote-first grounding, few diverse examples in `<example>` tags, explaining the why, role in system prompt (for tone/behavior; see Q3 for accuracy caveats).

### Inferences
- The consistent 2025–26 direction is "fewer, calmer, better-motivated instructions + architectural controls (effort, tool permissions, hooks, separate contexts)". For this harness, neutrality and calibration should be enforced by *structure* (collector cannot see the thesis owner's framing; judge is blind; hooks block cross-writes) rather than by emphatic prose.
- Because guidance is increasingly model-specific (Opus 5 verbose/over-verifying, Fable 5.1 dense prose and fewer updates, Sonnet 5 literal), the harness should record which model each subagent runs on and re-run a small eval set when models change; prompts tuned for one model can overtrigger on the next.
- The existing harness skills visible in this session (`analista-imparcial`, `pesquisador-de-mercado`) use "Use SEMPRE que…" and capitalized "NÃO"/"NUNCA"-style wording in descriptions. Per the overtriggering guidance, these are candidates for de-emphasis, validated with a triggering eval (see Q2).

### Gaps
- Anthropic has not published per-model multilingual numbers for Opus 5/5.5 or Fable models; the PT-BR figure is for Sonnet/Haiku 4.5.
- No Anthropic doc quantifies the effect of ALL-CAPS per se (the guidance is qualitative: overtriggering on Opus 4.5/4.6). I found no controlled public study of caps/emphasis on current Claude models.
- Token-cost overhead of Portuguese vs English prompts on Claude's tokenizer: no source found.

## 2. Writing skill and subagent descriptions that trigger correctly

### Takeaway
The description is the only thing the model sees when deciding to load a skill or delegate to a subagent, so it must say concretely *what* the component does and *when* to use it, in third person, with the words users actually type, and with the key use case first (listings get truncated). Anthropic's own sources pull in two directions — skill-creator says Claude tends to *under*trigger skills and recommends "a little bit pushy" descriptions, while the prompting guide says Opus 4.5+ *over*triggers on aggressive language — so the resolution is broad, concrete trigger coverage in calm language, verified by a triggering eval.

### Cited Findings
- "The `description` field enables Skill discovery and should include both what the Skill does and when to use it." "Always write in third person. The description is injected into the system prompt, and inconsistent point-of-view can cause discovery problems" (good: "Processes Excel files…"; avoid "I can help you…" / "You can use this…"). Claude uses it "to choose the right Skill from potentially 100+ available Skills." Vague descriptions ("Helps with documents") are anti-patterns. — [Skill authoring best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: Portuguese descriptions should be third-person declaratives ("Coleta evidências de mercado… Use quando…").
- Frontmatter limits: `name` ≤64 chars, lowercase/numbers/hyphens; `description` ≤1,024 chars, no XML tags; SKILL.md body under 500 lines; keep reference files one level deep (nested references get partially read with `head -100`-style previews). — [Skill authoring best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: move rubrics/templates into referenced files linked directly from SKILL.md.

- Orchestrator→subagent briefs: "Each subagent needs an objective, an output format, guidance on the tools and sources to use, and clear task boundaries. - Tool descriptions: "Bad tool descriptions can send agents down completely wrong paths, so each tool needs a distinct purpose and a clear description"; namespacing tools defines boundaries; use evals and let Claude optimize its own tool descriptions. — [Multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Writing effective tools for agents (Sep 11, 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents) · [Anthropic eng. blog] · Implication: the same "distinct purpose" rule applies to skills and subagents.
- Skill body style: "Default assumption: Claude is already very smart. Only add context Claude doesn't already have"; set "degrees of freedom" to task fragility (text heuristics when many approaches are valid, exact scripts when fragile); "Avoid offering too many options" (give a default with an escape hatch); "Avoid time-sensitive information"; "Use consistent terminology"; "Build evaluations first" (three scenarios, baseline without the skill); test on every model you'll use ("What works perfectly for Opus might need more detail for Haiku"). — [Skill authoring best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: keep one glossary of terms (e.g., "evidência", "sinal", "tese") used identically across all skills.

### Inferences
- The undertrigger (skill-creator) vs overtrigger (prompting guide) tension is probably model- and context-dependent: undertriggering is about *coverage* (missing trigger phrases), overtriggering about *intensity* (caps, "always", "if in doubt"). Writing descriptions that list many concrete trigger situations in neutral language addresses both.
- For a harness with role-separated skills (collect vs judge vs design), the main risk is *misrouting* between sibling skills. Each description should include a short "not for X (use Y)" clause; the existing skills already do this ("esta skill julga, não coleta"), which fits the guidance.
- Because the listing truncates to 1,536 chars and drops the least-used skills' descriptions first, rarely used but important skills (e.g., the blind judge) may lose their descriptions in a skill-heavy session; explicit invocation from the orchestrator or CLAUDE.md is more reliable than auto-triggering for critical pipeline steps.

### Gaps
- No public quantitative data on trigger precision/recall for Portuguese vs English descriptions.
- Skill-creator's "undertrigger" claim is undated in the file; I could not determine which model generation it was written against.

## 3. Persona / role prompting: does it help accuracy? Do trait personas help or hurt?

### Takeaway
Across 2024–2026 studies, expert personas ("você é um especialista em X") do not reliably improve factual or reasoning accuracy, can hurt knowledge-retrieval accuracy, and make outputs sensitive to irrelevant persona details; they mainly change style (more jargon and depth, less clarity) and can help alignment-type goals (tone, format, safety). Anthropic still recommends a one-line role for focus and tone. I found no direct study of *trait* personas ("crítico implacável", "visionário") on calibration; by analogy they mostly inject stance, which is risky for a harness that must stay neutral.

### Cited Findings
- Zheng, Pei, Logeswaran, Lee, Jurgens, "When 'A Helpful Assistant' Is Not Really Helpful: Personas in System Prompts Do Not Improve Performances of Large Language Models" (originally titled "Is 'A Helpful Assistant' the Best Role for Large Language Models?"; arXiv v1 Nov 2023, retitled revision dated 2024 per the aggregator): 162 personas (interpersonal roles + expertise domains), 4 LLM families, 2,410 factual questions; "adding personas to system prompts does not consistently improve model performance compared to a control setting with no persona"; automatically choosing the best persona per question was hard, often little better than a random choice. — [arXiv 2311.10054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054); summary via [aimodels.fyi](https://www.aimodels.fyi/papers/arxiv/when-helpful-assistant-is-not-really-helpful) · [Preprint; I believe published in Findings of EMNLP 2024 but could not open the ACL page to confirm] · Implication: don't expect "Você é um analista sênior de mercado" to raise accuracy; spend the tokens on task context.
- Wharton Generative AI Labs, "Prompting Science Report 4: Playing Pretend: Expert Personas Don't Improve Factual Accuracy" (Basil, Shapiro, Shapiro, Mollick, Mollick, Meincke; Dec 2025): on GPQA Diamond (198 questions) and MMLU-Pro (300 questions) with GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, o3-mini, o4-mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash, matched expert personas had no significant effect (except Gemini 2.0 Flash); low-knowledge personas ("Toddler", "Layperson") consistently decreased accuracy, scaling with implied ignorance. No Claude models tested. — [Wharton GAIL report page](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/playing-pretend-expert-personas/); [arXiv 2512.05858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05858) · [Tech report / Preprint] · Implication: personas that imply *limits* can degrade quality; avoid framings that diminish the model (e.g., "você é um estagiário").
- Luz de Araujo, Röttger, Hovy, Roth, "Principled Personas" (EMNLP 2025 main): "Models are highly sensitive to irrelevant persona details, with performance drops of almost 30 percentage points"; education/specialization/domain-relatedness effects "often inconsistent or negligible"; mitigation strategies "only work for the largest, most capable models." — [ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.1364](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1364/); [arXiv 2508.19764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19764) · [Peer-reviewed] · Implication: colorful persona details (name, age, backstory, temperament) are a source of variance, not capability; omit them.
- "Expert Personas Improve LLM Alignment but Damage Accuracy: Bootstrapping Intent-Based Persona Routing with PRISM" (USC, Mar 2026): persona effect is task-type dependent — "expert prompts consistently improve alignment-dependent tasks (safety, preference)" and format-following generation but "reliably damage pretraining-dependent knowledge retrieval" (e.g., MMLU); proposes routing personas by intent via a gated LoRA. — [arXiv 2603.18507](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18507v1); press coverage [The Register, 2026-03-24](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/03/24/telling-an-ai-model-that-its-an-expert-makes-it-worse/5226049) · [Preprint; Secondary] · Implication: a role line may help the offer/MVP designer's tone and format but should be kept out of fact-retrieval and probability-estimation prompts.
- "When Does Persona Prompting Actually Help? A Retrieval and Metric Analysis of Expert Role Injection in LLMs" (arXiv, May 2026): aggregate differences small; "role prompting systematically increases expertise depth while reducing clarity"; role prompts do best on advisory questions in medicine/psychology, while baseline does better on conceptual/explanatory questions in finance, legal, science and technology; "Persona prompting primarily reshapes response characteristics rather than broadly improving capability." — [arXiv 2605.29420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29420) · [Preprint] · Implication: for business/finance explanations (this harness's domain), personas likely cost readability without gaining accuracy.
- Anthropic's official stance: "Setting a role in the system prompt focuses Claude's behavior and tone for your use case. Even a single sentence makes a difference" (example: "You are a helpful coding assistant specializing in Python."). — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: consistent with the research if read as "role = focus/tone," not "role = accuracy"; one short functional role line is fine.
- Analogous evidence on stance instructions (not personas): Opus 5 follows "only report high-severity issues" / "be conservative" literally and reports less; Anthropic recommends reporting everything and filtering in a separate pass. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: a "ruthless critic" framing will likely suppress or skew findings rather than improve calibration; ask for complete, symmetric evidence and apply a separate calibrated judge.
- Anthropic's research-prompt pattern for calibration uses process, not persona: "develop several competing hypotheses. Track your confidence levels… to improve calibration. Regularly self-critique." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: replace "seja cético" with explicit procedures (steelman both sides, list disconfirming evidence, state probability with reasons).

### Inferences
- Synthesis: role/persona text reliably moves *style and stance* and sometimes *alignment-type* behavior; it does not reliably move *accuracy*, and irrelevant or diminishing details can hurt. For this harness: (a) collector prompts should use a functional role at most ("Sua função nesta etapa é coletar e registrar evidências, sem julgar"); (b) the judge should get no temperament persona, only a procedure and rubric; (c) the offer/MVP designer can use a role for tone and format.
- Trait personas ("crítico implacável", "visionário", "advogado do diabo") are best treated as *stance injections*. In an advocate/skeptic/blind-judge design the adversarial roles are legitimate *only* if their outputs go to a judge who sees both sides symmetrically and does not know which side produced which argument; the judge itself should carry no trait persona. This is an inference; I found no controlled study of trait personas on forecasting calibration.
- None of the persona studies tested current Claude models (Opus 5.x/Fable); Principled Personas found mitigations work only on the largest models, so frontier Claude may be *less* sensitive, but that is untested.

### Gaps
- Kim et al. 2024, "Persona is a Double-edged Sword" (arXiv 2408.08631), which I recall reports role-play prompts hurting reasoning on some datasets and proposes ensembling persona and neutral outputs: I could not retrieve it (arXiv blocked, search budget exhausted). Verify before citing.
- No study found on trait/temperament personas (critic, optimist, visionary) and probability calibration or forecasting accuracy; no study on multi-agent debate personas specific to Claude.
- No persona study run in Portuguese.

## 4. Structured outputs / JSON adherence; few-shot vs zero-shot; explicit CoT on reasoning models

### Takeaway
On current Claude models, JSON schema adherence is a solved API problem: Structured Outputs (GA) uses constrained decoding and guarantees schema-valid output, with known limits (no numeric/length constraints, all objects `additionalProperties: false`, required fields emitted first) and one key incompatibility (it cannot be combined with the Citations API). Academic work (2024) found strict format constraints can degrade reasoning, but on Claude the grammar applies only to the final output while thinking stays free, which likely shrinks that cost. Few-shot examples remain Anthropic's recommendation for format/tone; for reasoning accuracy on thinking models, zero-shot plus a clear problem statement is the safer default, and explicit CoT instructions add little on reasoning models (and can trigger refusals on Opus 5.5).

### Cited Findings

**Structured outputs**
- "Structured outputs guarantee schema-compliant responses through constrained decoding: Always valid… Type safe… No retries needed for schema violations"; two modes: JSON outputs (`output_config.format`) and strict tool use (`strict: true`); status GA; supported on Fable 5.1/5, Mythos, Opus 5.5/5/4.8/4.7/4.6/4.5, Sonnet 5/4.6/4.5, Haiku 4.5. — [Structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) · [Official doc] · Implication: use schema-enforced output for machine-read artifacts (evidence records, verdict JSON) instead of "responda apenas em JSON" prose.
- Limits: supported: basic types, `enum`, `const`, `anyOf`/`allOf` (limited), `$ref`/`$defs`, some string formats, `minItems` 0/1 only; not supported: recursive schemas, numeric constraints (`minimum`, `maximum`, `multipleOf`), `minLength`/`maxLength`, `additionalProperties` other than `false`; SDKs strip unsupported constraints and move them into field descriptions. — [Structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) · [Official doc] · Implication: a probability field can't be schema-bounded to [0,1]; validate range in code or use an enum of buckets.
- "Properties in objects maintain their defined ordering from your schema, with one important caveat: required properties appear first, followed by optional properties." — [Structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) · [Official doc] · Implication: if a justification field should precede the probability, make both required and order them that way.
- "Grammars apply only to Claude's direct output, not to tool use calls, tool results, or thinking… allowing Claude to think freely while still producing structured output in the final response." — [Structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) · [Official doc] · Implication: reasoning quality is protected by thinking even when the final answer is schema-constrained.
- Incompatibilities: Citations + `output_config.format` → 400 error ("citations require interleaving citation blocks with text"); prefilling incompatible with JSON outputs; first use of a schema adds grammar-compilation latency (cached 24h); an injected system prompt adds tokens; changing the format invalidates the prompt cache. — [Structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs); [Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) · [Official doc] · Implication: pipeline split — collect with citations (grounded prose), then convert to schema in a separate call.
- Without the feature: "Try asking the model to conform to your output structure first, as newer models can reliably match complex schemas when told to, especially if implemented with retries. For classification tasks, use either tools with an enum field… or structured outputs." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: in Claude Code (no API-level schema), specify the schema in the skill plus a validator script with a retry loop.
- Tam et al., "Let Me Speak Freely? A Study on the Impact of Format Restrictions on Performance of Large Language Models" (EMNLP 2024 Industry): "a significant decline in LLMs' reasoning abilities under format restrictions," with stricter constraints degrading more. — [ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-industry.91](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.91/) · [Peer-reviewed] (tested 2024-era non-thinking models) · Implication: don't force the *reasoning* into JSON; let the model reason first (thinking or free text), then emit the structured record.
- A 2026 preprint titled "Capacity, Not Format: Rethinking Structured Reasoning Failures" appeared in search results and may dispute the format-tax framing, but I could not read it. — [arXiv 2606.09410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09410) · [Preprint, unverified] · Implication: treat the format tax as plausible but model-dependent; measure on your own tasks.

**Few-shot vs zero-shot**
- Anthropic: examples "improve accuracy and consistency"; 3–5 diverse examples; "Multishot examples work with thinking. Use `<thinking>` tags inside your few-shot examples to show Claude the reasoning pattern." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: examples are endorsed on current Claude, mainly for output shape.
- Anthropic engineering: few-shot is "a well known best practice that we continue to strongly advise," but use "diverse, canonical examples," not "a laundry list of edge cases." — [Effective context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) · [Anthropic eng. blog] · Implication: 2–3 canonical examples of good evidence records/verdicts, not 20 edge cases.
- Fable 5.1: to stop unmarked verbatim reuse of retrieved sources, "add one complete example of a correct response to the system prompt: the user's request, the response, and a sentence explaining why the response is correct." — [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) · [Official doc] · Implication: an example with a short *rationale* is a strong steering tool on the newest models.
- Reasoning-model counter-evidence: DeepSeek-R1's authors report few-shot prompting "consistently degrades" R1 and recommend zero-shot problem description + output format; similar degradation was reported for OpenAI o1-preview. — [PromptHub: Prompt engineering with reasoning models](https://www.prompthub.us/blog/prompt-engineering-with-reasoning-models) · [Secondary] (primary: DeepSeek-R1 paper, arXiv 2501.12948, and Microsoft's Medprompt→o1 study, not retrieved) · Implication: for judgment/probability tasks, don't include worked examples of *reasoning*; describe the problem and criteria clearly.

**Explicit chain-of-thought on reasoning models**
- Wharton "Prompting Science Report 2: The Decreasing Value of Chain of Thought in Prompting" (Meincke, Mollick, Mollick, Shapiro; Jun 2025): non-reasoning models gain modestly on average but with more answer variability (CoT can break "easy" questions); reasoning models gain "only marginal benefits despite substantial time costs (20-80% increase)"; many models do CoT-like reasoning by default. — [Wharton GAIL](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/tech-report-chain-of-thought/); [arXiv 2506.07142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07142) · [Tech report / Preprint] · Implication: drop generic "pense passo a passo" from judge prompts.
- Anthropic (Opus 5.5): remove "think carefully" lines in chat (faster, no clear quality drop); remove instructions that make the model write out reasoning in its response (`reasoning_extraction` refusals); guidance on thinking is "promptable" but effort is the primary lever. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5); [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: ask for a structured *justification* (evidence pro/contra, key uncertainties) as an output artifact, not a transcript of reasoning.

### Inferences
- A good harness pattern given these constraints: (1) collector outputs grounded prose/records with citations; (2) a conversion step emits schema-valid JSON; (3) judge reasons in thinking, outputs a short justification plus probability in a schema; (4) code validates ranges and required fields. This avoids the citations/structured-output 400 conflict and the format tax on reasoning.
- The few-shot conflict resolves by purpose: examples for *format, tone, quoting discipline* (Anthropic endorses) vs no worked-reasoning examples for *judgment accuracy* (reasoning-model evidence). Examples can also anchor content (a sample verdict of "60%" can pull estimates), so calibration examples should vary outcomes widely or be omitted.
- In Claude Code (no `output_config.format` for the agent's own text), schema adherence depends on instructions + validation; newer models "reliably match complex schemas when told to," so a validator + retry is enough.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark of JSON-schema adherence *without* constrained decoding for Opus 5.x/Fable.
- No study found of the format tax on thinking-enabled Claude models specifically.
- Could not verify details for the "Ray (2026)" validity-vs-accuracy trade-off (reported by a search summary as up to −8.7 pp accuracy under hard schema enforcement) or "Zhou (2026) structure snowballing"; no URLs recovered, so treat as unverified.
- No Claude-specific study of few-shot vs zero-shot on reasoning accuracy.

## 5. Prompt patterns that reduce hallucinated facts in research tasks

### Takeaway
Anthropic's current anti-hallucination toolkit: explicitly allow "I don't know"; ground in documents via quote-first extraction; require a supporting quote per claim and retract unsupported claims; restrict to provided sources where appropriate; use the Citations API (guaranteed valid pointers); force verification of names and fast-moving facts via search rather than memory; and on long runs, audit every progress claim against a tool result. Newer models (Opus 5.5) are already much less likely to state wrong figures or cite the wrong source, but these patterns remain the main levers, and source-quality heuristics are needed to avoid SEO content farms.

### Cited Findings
- "Allow Claude to say 'I don't know': Explicitly give Claude permission to admit uncertainty. This simple technique can drastically reduce false information." Example: "If you're unsure… or if the report lacks necessary information, say 'I don't have enough information to confidently assess this.'" — [Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) · [Official doc] · Implication: give the collector an explicit "não encontrado / evidência insuficiente" output value that counts as a valid result.
- "For tasks involving long documents (>20k tokens), ask Claude to extract word-for-word quotes first before performing its task"; "If you can't find relevant quotes, state 'No relevant quotes found.'"; "Only base your analysis on the extracted quotes." — [Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations); same pattern with `<quotes>`/`<info>` tags in [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: evidence cards should carry the verbatim excerpt + URL + access date before any paraphrase.
- "Verify with citations… have Claude verify each claim by finding a supporting quote after it generates a response. If it can't find a quote, it must retract the claim" (mark removals with `[]`); "External knowledge restriction: Explicitly instruct Claude to only use information from provided documents"; "Best-of-N verification: … Inconsistencies across outputs could indicate hallucinations." — [Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) · [Official doc] · Implication: the judge should consider only claims in the evidence dossier; claims without a quote get dropped, not guessed.
- The same page still lists "Chain-of-thought verification: Ask Claude to explain its reasoning step-by-step before giving a final answer." — [Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) · [Official doc] · Implication: FLAG as likely outdated for always-thinking models: it conflicts with Opus 5.5's `reasoning_extraction` guidance ([Opus 5.5 guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)); prefer claim-level quote verification.
- Citations API: "citations are guaranteed to contain valid pointers to the provided documents"; `cited_text` does not count toward output tokens (or input tokens when passed back); incompatible with structured outputs. — [Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) · [Official doc] · Implication: if the harness calls the API directly for summarizing fetched pages, citations are cheaper and more reliable than prompted quoting.
- Investigate before answering (agentic): "Never speculate about code you have not opened… Never make any claims… before investigating unless you are certain of the correct answer." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: adapt to "não afirme fatos de mercado (preços, players, números) sem uma fonte aberta nesta sessão".
- Search triggering (Fable 5.1 at low effort answers from memory more often); fix: "When a query centers on a name you do not confidently recognize, or recognize from a fast-moving area… search before answering, and include the name as the user wrote it… partial background is exactly what makes an out-of-date answer sound authoritative, so familiarity is not a reason to skip the search." Also: raise effort for the affected turns. — [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) · [Official doc] · Implication: very relevant for company/price/competitor facts; put an adapted version in the collector skill and don't run collectors at the lowest effort.
- Ground progress claims: "Before reporting progress, audit each claim against a tool result from this session. Only report work you can point to evidence for; if something is not yet verified, say so explicitly." In Anthropic's testing "this nearly eliminated fabricated status reports even on tasks designed to elicit them." — [Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: apply the same audit rule to research summaries ("cada afirmação aponta para um registro de evidência").
- Quoting discipline: Fable 5.1 "is more likely… to reproduce passages of the source text without marking them as quotations"; fix with one complete correct example plus rationale. — [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) · [Official doc] · Implication: define explicitly when to quote verbatim (evidence cards) vs paraphrase (syntheses), with an example.
- Model-level improvement: Opus 5.5 "is much less likely to state an incorrect figure or cite the wrong source" and catches easy-to-miss details in large inputs. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: model choice for the collector matters; still keep grounding rules.
- Explore before acting: on Opus 5.5, one sentence telling the agent to list and open all potentially relevant sources, "including ones the task does not explicitly mention," let it complete "noticeably more" multi-app tasks correctly; caveat: "keep untrusted content out of the records it searches." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) · [Official doc] · Implication: tell collectors to check the project's existing notes/data before searching the web.
- Source quality: early research agents "consistently chose SEO-optimized content farms over authoritative but less highly-ranked sources like academic PDFs or personal blogs. Adding source quality heuristics to our prompts helped resolve this issue." — [Multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) · [Anthropic eng. blog] · Implication: the collector skill needs an explicit source-tier heuristic (primary data/regulators/filings > trade press > blogs/SEO pages).
- Calibrated research process: "develop several competing hypotheses. Track your confidence levels in your progress notes to improve calibration." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) · [Official doc] · Implication: have the judge record competing hypotheses and confidence per hypothesis, not a single narrative.
- Caveat from Anthropic: "while these techniques significantly reduce hallucinations, they don't eliminate them entirely. Always validate critical information." — [Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) · [Official doc] · Implication: keep a human or programmatic spot-check on figures that drive go/no-go decisions.

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern set for a neutral evidence collector is: permission to report "não encontrado" + verbatim quote + URL/date per claim + source tier + no claims from memory on fast-moving entities + a final pass that retracts any claim without a supporting record. For the judge: consider only dossier claims, cite record IDs, and give a probability with the key uncertainties.
- Many "say I don't know" and quote-first patterns predate the 2025–26 models but remain in current docs and don't conflict with newer guidance; the one exception is prompting for written step-by-step reasoning, which now conflicts with always-on thinking and reasoning-extraction safeguards.

### Gaps
- No quantitative Anthropic data on how much "I don't know" permission or quote-first reduces hallucination on current models (the docs say "drastically" without numbers).
- No independent (non-Anthropic) 2025–26 study found comparing these grounding patterns on Claude specifically; search budget ran out before I could look for RAG-faithfulness benchmarks on Claude 5-series models.
