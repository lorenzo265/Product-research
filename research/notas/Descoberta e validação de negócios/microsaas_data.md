## 1. Revenue distributions, base rates, time-to-revenue, churn by price point, B2B vs B2C

### Takeaway
Across every dataset found, the distribution is extremely skewed. Roughly two-thirds of revenue-generating indie products sit below US$1k MRR, and only ~15–20% clear ~US$6–10k MRR at any age. Reaching ~R$30k MRR is a top-quintile outcome among products that already have revenue, and rarer still among all attempts. Price point (ARPA) is the strongest published predictor of retention. Below ~US$10/month, customer churn runs ~6–7%/month. AI products under US$50/month keep only ~23% of revenue after a year.

### Cited Findings

**Revenue distributions (global/US)**

So ≥$1k ≈ 32%, ≥$10k ≈ 15.6%. — [TrustMRR stats](https://trustmrr.com/stats) · `type: verified-revenue directory | geo: global, X/indie-hacker audience skew | BR: med | tier: T1 data, but self-selected listings`. It was unclear whether buckets are MRR or trailing-30-day revenue, and the listing date was not visible. → *Implication:* use as the default prior. A random revenue-generating indie product has roughly a 1-in-6 chance of being at or above US$10k, so the R$30k target needs above-median evidence (ticket, channel) at the card stage.
- **Revenue distribution attributed in a search summary to the MicroConf 2024 context (attribution uncertain, possibly the lnks.work blog):** annual revenue <$10k: 38% · $10–50k: 28% · $50–120k: 18% · $120–300k: 10% · $300k–1M: 5% · >$1M: ~1%. — [MicroConf State of Indie SaaS](https://microconf.com/state-of-indie-saas); [lnks.work](https://lnks.work/blog/how-much-saas-founders-earn) · `type: founder survey (uncertain) | geo: global, US-heavy | BR: med | tier: T2/T3`. → *Implication:* this is consistent with TrustMRR (~16% ≥ $120k ARR ≈ $10k MRR). Two independent-looking sources agree on the shape.
- **MicroConf State of Independent SaaS 2024:** 469 bootstrapped SaaS respondents, surveyed late 2023. The single largest group is under $1k MRR. — [MicroConf report page](https://microconf.com/state-of-indie-saas) · `type: founder survey | geo: global, US/EU-heavy | BR: med | tier: T2`. → *Implication:* even among people self-identifying as SaaS founders (a survivor-skewed sample), sub-$1k is the modal state.
- **MicroConf 2025 conference audience:** 28% of 230 founders reported >$100k MRR, with no VC. — search summary citing [MicroConf](https://microconf.com/founders/100k-1m-arr) · `type: event audience | geo: US | BR: low | tier: T3, heavy selection bias`. → *Implication:* never use conference or mastermind audiences as base rates. They are survivors.
- **"~70% under $1k MRR; 18% at $1–5k; median profitable micro-SaaS ≈ $4.2k MRR; top 1% > $50k MRR"** appears in several SEO/aggregator posts and is attributed to the Freemius 2025 "State of Micro-SaaS". — [Freemius blog](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/); [SaaSRanger](https://saasranger.com/blog/micro-saas-revenue-reality-what-1000-founders-actually-earn/); [RockingWeb](https://www.rockingweb.com.au/micro-saas-revenue-analysis-2025/) · `type: vendor blog + SEO aggregators | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T3`. Ignore the $4.2k "median" as a planning number.
- **Freemius 2025:** 45.7% of SaaS makers selling on Freemius are solo founders. Also claims "95% of analyzed micro-SaaS reach profitability within their first year", which is implausible as a base rate and is likely conditioned on already-selling products with near-zero costs. — [Freemius blog](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/) · `type: vendor platform data | geo: global, WordPress-plugin skew | BR: med | tier: T3`. → *Implication:* "profitable" ≠ "meaningful revenue". The harness should gate on MRR level and trajectory, not profitability.

**Brazil-specific distributions**

Motivation: 73% want financial/time freedom, 21% want to become a startup. Caveat: 76% + 20% leaves only 4% between R$5k and R$15k, which is odd. The denominator (all respondents vs. only revenue-generating ones) is unclear. → *Implication:* the harness's R$30k floor is *above* the community's own definition of micro-SaaS (R$25k). That is a top-decile outcome in the local ecosystem, so Track M cards should be scored against it explicitly.
- **ListaMRR (Brazilian verified-MRR ranking):** 246 listed startups sum to R$514k verified MRR (+9.4% in the month shown). Data comes via read-only payment-gateway APIs across 9 gateways, and 103+ founders have connected a gateway. — [ListaMRR](https://listamrr.com.br/) · `type: verified-revenue directory | geo: Brazil | BR: high | tier: T1 data, self-selected, date not visible`. → *Implication:* the whole public verified Brazilian indie ecosystem was ≈17× R$30k combined. Mean ≈ R$2.1k per listed startup (≈ R$5k if only the 103 connected ones carry the revenue). R$30k MRR would rank near the top of this list.
- **Brazil SaaS market context:** US$7.9B in 2025, projected CAGR 13.87% to US$25.5B by 2034 ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/brazil-saas-market), `type: market-research firm | tier: T3`). Brazil ≈ 48% of the LatAm SaaS market, projected at US$46B by 2027 ([Finsiders/Acorn](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/giro-noticias/mercado-de-saas-na-america-latina-pode-atingir-us-46-bi-ate-2027-aponta-acorn), `tier: T3`). 263 SaaS M&A deals in Brazil in 2025 (+6%, US$2.1B) ([Negócios Brasil](https://negociosbrasil.com.br/quanto-vale-uma-empresa-saas-no-brasil/), `tier: T3, primary source not visible`). 53% of Brazilian SaaS brands analyzed mainly serve SMBs ([Leadster citing Superlogic](https://leadster.com.br/blog/mercado-saas/), `tier: T3`). → *Implication:* this is macro context only. It says nothing about indie odds, so don't let TAM-style numbers enter Track M scoring.

**Time to revenue**
- **Indie Hackers analysis of Stripe-verified revenue (n ≈ 30):** average 9 months to $1k MRR. P25 = 5 months, P50 = 8 months, P75 = 12 months, for those who got there. The author notes the revenue may not be recurring. — [Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/it-takes-5-months-to-reach-1k-in-mrr-491742f806); [IH "What's New"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/whats-new-how-long-does-it-take-to-hit-1k-mrr-35bda788b1) · `type: small-sample platform analysis | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T2, undated (likely pre-2021), survivor-only`. → *Implication:* even survivors took ~8 months to reach US$1k (≈ R$5k). An 18-month path to R$30k (≈6× that) requires either higher ticket from day one or an existing channel. A cold-start B2C tool rarely fits.
- **ChartMogul "Against the Odds" 2025 (6,525 software companies on ChartMogul):** ~half of monetizing SaaS reach $1M ARR within 10 years. 1 in 10 reach $10M and 1 in 50 reach $25M. For those that reach $1M, the typical path is 2–5 years, and only 3.3% get there in < 12 months. — [ChartMogul report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/); [Growth Unhinged summary](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-odds-of-making-it) · `type: billing-data benchmark | geo: global, US/EU | BR: med | tier: T1`. The sample is companies that already pay for a billing-analytics tool, so it is **strongly** survivor-skewed. → *Implication:* SaaS ramps are slow even among companies with traction. Track M's 18-month clock should be treated as ambitious, and cards should state why this one ramps faster (existing audience or channel, service revenue, ecosystem distribution).
- **ChartMogul Bootstrapped vs VC (2024):** top-quartile bootstrapped companies reach $1M ARR in ~2 years, only ~4 months slower than VC-backed. At $1–30M ARR, bootstrapped grew 44% YoY vs 42.8% for VC. CAC was ~$5k (bootstrapped) vs ~$21k (VC). Below $1M ARR, retention is equal across funding models. — [ChartMogul report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-vc-bootstrapped/) · `type: billing-data benchmark | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T1`. → *Implication:* funding doesn't buy retention early. At the micro stage, retention depends on product and market fit, which supports scoring ICP/ticket over "resources".
- **Stripe (annual letters, 2024–2025):** the top 100 AI companies on Stripe reached $1M annualized revenue in a median 11.5 months, ~4 months ahead of the fastest SaaS cohort. They reached $5M in 24 months (2024 cohort) vs 37 months for top-100 SaaS (2018). 20% of new Stripe Atlas incorporations began charging within 30 days, up from 8% in 2020. The 2025 startup cohort grew 50% faster than the prior year. — [Stripe 2025 annual letter PDF](https://assets.stripeassets.com/fzn2n1nzq965/3LlGw839Q6kUwxZlLZDtH6/75ddcbada4aa7743dd8ec7d0f9ca497e/Stripe-annual-letter-2025-desktop.pdf); [SaaStr summary](https://www.saastr.com/stripes-latest-data-startups-are-growing-50-faster-computer-demand-drove-50-of-gdp-growth-60-more-apps-yoy-and-more/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/27/stripe-ceo-says-ai-startups-are-growing-faster-than-saas-ever-did-and-calling-them-wrappers-misses-the-point) · `type: payment-processor data | geo: global, US-heavy | BR: med | tier: T1 for the stats, but the "top 100" figures are extreme outliers`. → *Implication:* "charge within 30 days" is now common (1 in 5 new companies). The Track M kill rule "MVP > 4 weeks" is consistent with current norms, and the harness can demand paid validation inside 30 days.
- **Low-tier claims (listed so they are recognized, not used):** "$0 → $10k MRR takes 1–3 years for products that reach it" ([Unbuilt Lab](https://unbuiltlab.com/blog/indie-hacker-stripe-milestones-saas-revenue.html)). "$10k MRR solo typically 9–18 months, fastest 6–9" (Brazilian-language summary of blogs). "Growing share of bootstrapped SaaS reach MVP/early revenue in < 90 days; 30–60 days common" (search summary, source unclear). — `tier: T3/T4`. → *Implication:* none of these should set gates.

**Churn by price point / ARPA; B2B vs B2C**
- **ChartMogul retention data (2023 report + benchmark blog, 2,100+ SaaS businesses):** with ARPA < $10/month, expect 6–7% monthly customer churn. Top-quartile NRR in that band is only 65.1%, and just 2.7% of those businesses have NRR > 100%. Top quartile retains 87% of customers in the first 3 months at ASP < $10. — [ChartMogul Retention Report 2023](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-report/); [ChartMogul benchmarks blog](https://chartmogul.com/blog/retention-benchmarks-and-insights/) · `type: billing-data benchmark | geo: global | BR: med-high (the mechanism of low commitment transfers; Brazilian SMB ticket tolerance is lower, so more products fall into low-ARPA bands)` · `tier: T1`. → *Implication:* a R$49/month ticket (≈ US$9–10) sits in the worst retention band. Track M should flag tickets below ~R$100–150/month as high-churn unless the card shows a switching-cost mechanism.
- **ChartMogul "The AI churn wave" (2025; ~2,700 B2B SaaS, ~600 B2C SaaS, ~200 AI-native):** AI-native products average ~40% GRR. By price: < $50/month → 23% GRR; $50–249/month → 45% GRR; > $250/month → 70% GRR and 85% NRR, "like B2B SaaS". **Conflict:** one summary gives B2B SaaS GRR as 82%, another says 63% "for B2B SaaS in the same sample". 63% may be the B2C figure; unresolved. — [ChartMogul report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/); [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-ai-churn-wave) · `type: billing-data benchmark | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T1`. → *Implication:* an "AI tool" at under ~R$250/month loses most of its revenue within a year. Track M should require either ticket ≥ ~R$250/month or workflow lock-in (integrations, data, habit) for any AI-centric card.
- **Recurly 2025 churn benchmarks:** B2B SaaS average monthly churn 3.5% (2.6% voluntary + 0.8% involuntary). Another cut gives B2B software 3.36% voluntary + 1.19% involuntary, vs B2C SaaS 4.0% voluntary + 1.70% involuntary. A blog synthesis gives enterprise 1–2%/month, mid-market 1.5–3%, SMB/prosumer 3–5%. — [Recurly churn benchmarks](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/); [Vitally](https://www.vitally.io/post/saas-churn-benchmarks) · `type: billing-platform benchmark (T1) + vendor blog (T3) | geo: US/global | BR: med`. → *Implication:* budget ≥ 3–5% monthly churn for SMB-facing micro-SaaS. At 4%, holding R$30k MRR needs ~R$1.2k of new MRR every month just to stand still.
- **Churn arithmetic (opinion + math):** "5% monthly churn kills a micro-SaaS", since 1 − 0.95¹² ≈ 46% of customers are lost per year. — search summary under MicroConf 2024 context · `tier: T4 opinion, math verified`. → *Implication:* the harness should compute the steady-state ceiling for each card (new MRR per month ÷ churn rate) and kill cards whose plausible acquisition rate can't support R$30k at realistic churn.
- **Brazil payment-driven involuntary churn:** Pix Automático went live on 16 June 2025 for authorized recurring debits ([PagBrasil](https://www.pagbrasil.com/pt-br/blog/pagamento-recorrente/pix-automatico-2026/); [Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/pix-automatico-pagamentos-recorrentes-sem-cartao-credito)). Adoption grew strongly from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 ([PagBrasil](https://www.pagbrasil.com/pt-br/blog/pagamento-recorrente/pix-automatico-2026/)). Vendor blogs claim recurring-card failure rates of 8–14% of attempts vs 2–5% for Pix Automático, and 10–20% of SaaS MRR lost to involuntary churn ([KMEE](https://kmee.com.br/blog/pix-automatico-saas-mensalidade-recorrente/); [Nexforce](https://nexforce.ai/blog/pix-automatico-pagamentos-recorrentes-saas)). — `type: payment-vendor blogs | geo: Brazil | BR: high | tier: T3 (vendors selling the solution)`. → *Implication:* Brazil-first cards should assume higher involuntary churn than US benchmarks (Recurly B2C involuntary is already 1.7%/month) and plan Pix Automático/boleto billing from day one. This is a real cost of the Brazil side of geographic arbitrage.

### Inferences
- **Base rate for "≈US$6k (R$30k) MRR" among revenue-generating indie products, at any age:** interpolating TrustMRR's $1k–10k bucket log-uniformly gives ≈19% at or above $6k. A Pareto shape would put it closer to ~15%. The MicroConf-context distribution gives ~22–26% at or above ~$72k ARR. **Working prior: ~15–25% of products *with revenue* ever reach R$30k-equivalent.** The rate is lower, plausibly under 10%, within an 18-month window, and much lower across all attempts, since 68% of Brazilian community respondents haven't launched. All of this is inference from biased, snippet-level data.
- **Brazil is a thinner tail.** ListaMRR's total verified MRR (R$514k across 246 startups) and the community's own R$25k ceiling suggest a Brazil-only product at R$30k MRR is rarer than the US equivalent at US$6k. That supports the arbitrage angle: sell to US/EU buyers or price in USD.
- **Minimum ticket math for R$30k MRR:** R$99/month → ~303 customers; R$199 → ~151; R$499 → ~60. At SMB churn of 3–5%/month, the ~150-customer case needs roughly 5–8 new customers per month just to stand still. A channel "with an address" must plausibly deliver ~10–20 new paying customers per month by months 6–12 for the 18-month plan to work.
- Price-band evidence (ChartMogul, both general SaaS and AI) is the single strongest quantitative lever the harness can score at card stage. The ticket × churn interaction dominates outcomes more than idea novelty does.

### Gaps
- Could not open any primary report: the MicroConf 2024 PDF (full revenue distribution, churn by price, solo share), ChartMogul PDFs, Acquire.com PDFs, the Freemius 2025 report, or TrustMRR's country/Brazil page. **All numbers need verification before becoming hard thresholds.**
- No retrieved MicroConf 2025/2026 edition. Unknown whether one was published.
- No reliable data on the **time distribution** to US$6k MRR specifically, or on base rates across *all attempts* rather than products with revenue.
- Paddle/ProfitWell and Baremetrics benchmarks (churn by ARPU, B2B vs B2C) were not retrieved: the search budget ran out and fetches were blocked.
- No Brazil data on churn by ticket, B2B SMB willingness to pay, or time-to-revenue. The Brazilian community survey is undated and has ambiguous denominators.

## 2. Where successful founders' ideas come from (own pain, service business, audience, ecosystems, copying)

### Takeaway
No rigorous 2023–2026 survey data on idea origins for indie SaaS was found. The field is anecdote-driven (Starter Story, Indie Hackers interviews, "scratch your own itch" narratives). The strongest structured signals are indirect: ecosystem distribution (Stair Step), the prevalence of operator-pain stories in case databases, and older academic work on prior knowledge and prior employment, which this session could not verify. The harness should treat idea origin as a weak prior and score the *channel and buyer evidence* that each origin tends to bring with it.

### Cited Findings
- **Starter Story** has 4,400+ revenue-verified case studies covering how founders got the idea and their first 100 customers. It publishes no aggregate breakdown of idea origins in what was retrieved. One example: PropertyData (property-data analytics) came from the founder's own manual, error-prone analysis as an investor and is reported at ~$128k/month. — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/); [1Capture review](https://www.1capture.io/blog/starter-story-review); [Starter Story data-analysis stories](https://www.starterstory.com/ideas/data-analysis-business/success-stories) · `type: case database / anecdote | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T4 for origin claims, survivorship-biased by design`. → *Implication:* "own pain as an operator" is the dominant *narrative*. The harness should ask whether the founder or an accessible insider has operator knowledge of the workflow, since that knowledge also supplies the channel.
- **Qualitative consensus in bootstrapper writing:** "scratch your own itch / build for yourself / fix what you know". Initial solutions are often not software, and founders often weren't planning a company. — [Founders Confidential, "I profiled 80 companies for indie…"](https://foundersconfidential418.substack.com/p/i-profiled-80-companies-for-indie) (attribution from search summary; post not opened) · `type: qualitative profiling / opinion | geo: US | BR: med | tier: T4`. → *Implication:* supports the harness's Service → Micro-SaaS path. The manual/service version is often the origin.
- **Stair Step examples as idea sources:** Rob Walling's Step 1 examples are add-ons in existing ecosystems (WordPress plugins/themes, Shopify apps, Heroku add-ons, Magento/Drupal add-ons, Photoshop plugins, ebooks/courses), i.e. ideas found *inside a platform's demand*. — [MicroConf Stair Step article](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping); [Walling 2015 essay](https://robwalling.com/essays/2015/03/26/the-stair-step-method-of-bootstrapping) · `type: practitioner framework | geo: US | BR: med`. Brazilian equivalents exist but were not researched. → *Implication:* "platform gap" is a valid Track M idea source because it comes with a built-in channel, the app-store listing.
- **Freemius 2025:** "68% of successful solo founders used AI primarily for development acceleration, not as the core product feature". — [Freemius blog](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/) · `type: vendor survey | tier: T3, method unclear, "successful" undefined`. → *Implication:* weak evidence that "AI-as-product" is not what distinguishes winners. It is consistent with the ChartMogul AI churn data in Q1.
- **Brazilian micro-SaaS founders' motivations:** 73% want financial/time freedom, 21% want to become a startup. This is motivation, not idea origin. — [Micro-SaaS.com.br](https://microsaas.com.br/mercado-brasileiro-de-micro-saas/) · `type: community survey | geo: Brazil | BR: high | tier: T2`. → *Implication:* most local founders optimize for lifestyle. The competitive density of ambitious local micro-SaaS players in a niche may be lower than the product count suggests.

### Inferences
- Idea origins that bring **their own distribution** tend to cluster in success stories: operator pain (industry access), service business (paying clients already), audience (followers), and ecosystem gap (app-store traffic). That is consistent with Track M's emphasis on a "canal com endereço". Scoring should reward the *channel that comes with the origin*, not the origin label itself.
- "Copying proven products" (clone plus niche or geography) fits geographic arbitrage: bringing a proven US tool to Brazilian SMBs, or selling a Brazilian-built tool abroad in USD. No data was found on its success rate.

### Gaps
- **No survey data found** (2023–2026) that quantifies idea origins for indie or micro-SaaS founders. The search budget ran out before targeted queries on Indie Hackers or MicroConf idea-origin questions.
- **Recalled from background knowledge, NOT verified this session:** Amar Bhidé's *The Origin and Evolution of New Businesses* (2000) reported that ~71% of Inc. 500 founders replicated or modified an idea encountered in previous employment. Scott Shane (2000, *Organization Science*) showed that entrepreneurs discover opportunities related to their prior knowledge. Both are old, US, and not micro-SaaS-specific. Verify before citing.
- No data comparing success rates by idea origin (e.g., operator pain vs. audience-first vs. ecosystem gap).

## 3. Stair Step approach, platform ecosystems (Shopify, WordPress), and "boring" vertical niches

### Takeaway
The Stair Step approach (Walling) is a practitioner framework, not a validated finding. It recommends starting with one-time-sale, single-channel products inside ecosystems before building standalone SaaS, because SaaS ramps are long. Ecosystem economics are currently favorable (Shopify takes 0% of the first US$1M), but ecosystem revenue is extremely concentrated. Most WordPress/CodeCanyon plugins and most Shopify apps earn very little. No quantitative data was found on "boring vertical niches".

### Cited Findings
- **Stair Step method:** Step 1 is a one-time-sale, single-channel product (plugins, apps, themes, ebooks). Step 2 is repeating Step 1 until it replaces job income. Step 3 is recurring-revenue SaaS, because "there's a long ramp to substantial revenue with SaaS". The stated goal is to build reserves and buy back time. — [MicroConf](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping); [Walling essay (2015)](https://robwalling.com/essays/2015/03/26/the-stair-step-method-of-bootstrapping); [The Bootstrapped Founder](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/rob-walling-stair-stepping-into-saas-success/) · `type: practitioner framework | geo: US | BR: med-high (logic transfers; the example ecosystems are US-centric) | tier: T4 (framework from observation, no data); 2015 origin, flag as older`. → *Implication:* for Track M, a platform add-on can be a legitimate step toward R$30k, with a smaller ceiling but faster first revenue. The harness could allow a "Step 1/2" sub-path when a card's standalone SaaS channel is weak.
- **Shopify revenue share (primary):** since 1 Jan 2025, developers keep 100% of their first US$1M in lifetime gross app revenue and pay 15% above that, plus a 2.9% processing fee. Developers with US$20M+ Shopify App Store revenue in the prior year, or US$100M+ company revenue, pay 15% on everything. — [Shopify.dev revenue share](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share); [Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/update-to-shopifys-app-developer-revenue-share) · `type: primary platform policy | geo: global | BR: med (Shopify is used in Brazil, but see gap on Nuvemshop) | tier: T1`. → *Implication:* platform economics are not a blocker at micro scale (0% take below US$1M). The binding constraint is app-store discovery and competition.
- **Shopify App Store size and growth:** 16,846 apps and 40,556 registered developer partners. The app count grew ~71% between early 2024 and Q1 2026 (7,000+ new apps in two years). — [StackDex "State of Shopify Apps 2026"](https://www.stackdex.io/research/state-of-shopify-apps-2026); [Uptek](https://uptek.com/shopify-statistics/app-store/) · `type: third-party scrape/aggregator | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T3`. → *Implication:* ecosystem saturation is rising fast, consistent with AI-era lower build costs. Ecosystem cards need a differentiation/ranking analysis (reviews, category depth), not just "gap exists".
- **Shopify app revenue concentration:** "top 1% of apps clear $1M+ ARR, top 10% clear $100K+ ARR, median listed app earns under $1,000/month". Separately, "average Shopify app developer earns $93k/year; top 25% ~$167k", and "developers have collectively earned over $1.5B since inception". — [Week One Labs](https://weekonelabs.com/blog/shopify-app-revenue-benchmarks-2026/); [Uptek](https://uptek.com/shopify-statistics/app-store/); [ShopThemeDetector](https://shopthemedetector.com/blog/shopify-app-store-statistics/) · `type: blog/aggregator | tier: T3. Methodology unknown; the $93k/$167k figures appear to be recycled older stats of unclear origin`. The "median < $1k/month" claim matches the general indie shape but is not verified. → *Implication:* treat the ecosystem path as having the same skewed base rate as standalone indie SaaS. It is not a shortcut to R$30k.
- **WordPress plugins (Freemius data):** free-to-paid conversion of 1–3% is standard across plugins sold on Freemius. For freemium plugins at scale, ~70% of revenue comes from annual renewals. Just over 1% of WordPress.org plugins have > 10,000 active installs. In a CodeCanyon cohort analysis, 84% of plugins earned < $500 after taxes in their first year and only 5 earned > $10k. — [Freemius CodeCanyon analysis](https://freemius.com/blog/codecanyon-wordpress-plugins-analysis/); [Freemius, how WP developers make money](https://freemius.com/blog/how-wordpress-plugin-developers-make-money/) · `type: vendor platform data | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T2/T3; the CodeCanyon analysis is likely pre-2020, flag as old`. → *Implication:* use 1–3% free→paid as the default conversion prior for any freemium Track M card. Reaching ~150 paying customers at 2% needs ~7,500 active free users, and the channel must plausibly deliver that.

### Inferences
- Stair Step and ecosystem data point the same way: **distribution you can see (an app store, a plugin directory) beats a novel idea**, but ecosystems are winner-take-most. The harness should check, for any ecosystem card, whether the category's top apps have few or poor reviews, meaning the ranking is winnable.
- For Brazil-first, the relevant ecosystems are probably local (e.g., Nuvemshop, ERP/fiscal platforms, WhatsApp Business tooling). That is a hypothesis; it was not researched here.

### Gaps
- **"Boring" vertical niches:** no quantitative data found (the search budget ran out). The claim that boring verticals outperform is opinion/anecdote in the indie literature.
- No verified Shopify app revenue distribution from Shopify itself or a transparent scrape methodology.
- No data on Brazilian platform ecosystems (Nuvemshop app store size and revenue share, Bling/Omie/Conta Azul/RD Station/Hotmart/Kiwify marketplaces). **High priority for the Brazil-first track.**
- No Atlassian Marketplace, HubSpot, or Chrome Web Store monetization distributions.

## 4. What distinguishes survivors: channel, ACV/price, B2B vs B2C, niche size, solo vs team

### Takeaway
The best-supported differentiators are **price/ARPA** (billing data: retention improves sharply with ticket), **full-time focus** (MicroConf: 2.2× faster growth), and **B2B over B2C/prosumer** (lower voluntary and involuntary churn). Channel data is survey-level: SEO, word of mouth, communities, and integrations/partnerships are the most cited. Solo founding is increasingly common (36–46% depending on the sample) and not in itself a disadvantage at micro scale. No data isolates niche size.

### Cited Findings
- **Full-time vs part-time; developer-led teams (MicroConf 2024, n = 469, late-2023 data):** companies with full-time founders grow 2.2× faster than part-time. Developer-led teams grow 1.7× faster. — [MicroConf State of Indie SaaS](https://microconf.com/state-of-indie-saas) · `type: founder survey | geo: global, US-heavy | BR: high (the mechanism transfers) | tier: T2, correlation only (reverse causality likely: founders go full-time once it works)`. → *Implication:* the 18-month plan should state the operator's hours. A part-time operator should be modeled with a longer horizon or a lower target.
- **Customer segment and growth:** the same search summary claims enterprise-targeting companies showed the fastest month-over-month growth (~27%) vs single digits for SMB/consumer. — [MicroConf](https://microconf.com/state-of-indie-saas) · `tier: T2 but likely garbled in the summary (27% MoM is implausible as a typical figure). Do not use until verified`. → *Implication:* only directional. Higher-ACV buyers correlate with faster growth, which must be balanced against Track M's "sales cycle > 30 days" kill rule.
- **Channels (surveys):** founders name SEO and word of mouth as their highest-impact marketing. 47% say integrations, partnerships, communities, and forums became a more dependable growth source. 50% of independent SaaS founders lean primarily on communities and referrals, reportedly with higher LTV early on. — search summaries citing [MicroConf](https://microconf.com/state-of-indie-saas) and [Freemius 2025](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/) · `type: founder surveys | geo: global | BR: med (in Brazil, WhatsApp groups and Instagram likely replace forums; unverified) | tier: T2/T3, attribution between the two reports uncertain`. → *Implication:* "canal com endereço" is supported. Community, referral, and integration/partner channels are the most commonly reported working channels for indies. Paid acquisition barely appears.
- **Retention by ARPA (billing data):** see Q1. < $10 ARPA → 6–7% monthly churn and NRR rarely > 100%. AI > $250/month → 70% GRR. The best SaaS companies at $1M ARR grew ARPA by 82% and NRR by 10 points. — [ChartMogul AI churn wave](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/); [ChartMogul Retention 2023](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-report/) · `tier: T1 | BR: med`. → *Implication:* ticket is the most defensible quantitative filter available. It belongs in the kill-cheap step.
- **B2B vs B2C:** Recurly: B2C SaaS churns more voluntarily (4.0% vs ~2.6–3.4%/month) and involuntarily (1.7% vs 0.8–1.2%). The Brazilian micro-SaaS community skews B2B, with 30% selling to B2C. — [Recurly](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/); [Micro-SaaS.com.br](https://microsaas.com.br/mercado-brasileiro-de-micro-saas/) · `tier: T1/T2 | geo: US + Brazil`. → *Implication:* default Track M to B2B. B2C cards should carry a churn penalty and need a much larger reachable audience.
- **Solo vs team:** 45.7% of SaaS makers on Freemius are solo ([Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/), T3). A Portuguese-language summary cites 39% of independent SaaS founders as solo "per the 2024 report", likely MicroConf ([summary page](https://microsaas.com.br/mercado-brasileiro-de-micro-saas/), T3, attribution uncertain). Carta: solo-founded share of *new startups on Carta* rose from 23.7% (2019) to 36.3% (H1 2025). Solo founders hired their first employee at a median 399 days vs 480 for multi-founder teams. Solo-led companies were 30% of 2024 startups but received 14.7% of priced-round cash ([Carta Solo Founders Report 2025](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report/), T1 for Carta's population: VC-oriented US startups, BR low). → *Implication:* solo operation is normal among indies. The harness shouldn't penalize solo, but it should penalize support-heavy or sales-heavy cards, which is what actually breaks a solo operator.
- **Exit and value signals (Acquire.com):** SaaS sold at a median **3.9× profit** multiple on Acquire.com in both 2024 and 2025. Buyers rank profit margin first, then YoY growth, then time in business (3+ years is a strong signal). Asking prices exceed outcomes. — [Acquire.com biannual report (Jan 2026)](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/); [2025 findings recap](https://blog.acquire.com/acquisition-multiples-report-2025-findings-webinar-recap/) · `type: marketplace data | geo: global, US buyers | BR: low-med (US buyers may discount BRL revenue; the local acquirer pool is thin but exists, cf. 263 Brazilian SaaS M&A deals in 2025)` · `tier: T1/T2`. → *Implication:* a R$30k MRR business at ~70% margin (≈R$250k/yr profit) implies roughly R$1M at 3.9×. This matters for the portfolio view; the arithmetic is an inference.

### Inferences
- A defensible Track M scorecard can rank these differentiators by evidence quality: (1) ticket/ARPA (T1), (2) B2B vs B2C (T1), (3) founder time commitment (T2), (4) channel type (T2), (5) niche size (no data).
- Higher-ACV B2B grows faster but conflicts with the "sales cycle ≤ 30 days" rule. The sweet spot implied by the data is B2B SMB at roughly US$30–100/month (≈R$150–500), sold self-serve or with light touch through a channel that already gathers the buyers.

### Gaps
- No data isolating **niche size** (TAM of the niche) vs. success for indie SaaS.
- No data on **acquisition channel vs. outcome** from billing data. Channel evidence is survey-only.
- No Brazil-specific data on channels (WhatsApp, Instagram, accountants/"contadores" as a channel, marketplaces) for micro-SaaS.
- MicroConf's full cross-tabs (e.g., growth by price point, B2B vs B2C, solo vs team) were not retrievable.

## 5. AI-era changes (2024–2026): build speed, "AI wrapper" commoditization, vibe-coded competition, defensibility and pricing

### Takeaway
Build and launch speed has clearly increased. There are more apps, faster first charges, and faster top-end growth. Product supply is surging (App Store submissions +84% YoY in Q1 2026; Shopify apps +71% in two years). The retention data shows the cost: low-priced AI products keep very little revenue (23% GRR under US$50/month). Defensibility therefore shifts from "can build it" to channel, workflow depth, and price point. No hard data was found on indie price compression.

### Cited Findings
- **Faster to revenue:** 20% of new Stripe Atlas companies charge within 30 days (8% in 2020). The 2025 startup cohort grew 50% faster than the prior year. The top 100 AI companies reached $1M ARR in a median 11.5 months. — [Stripe letter](https://assets.stripeassets.com/fzn2n1nzq965/3LlGw839Q6kUwxZlLZDtH6/75ddcbada4aa7743dd8ec7d0f9ca497e/Stripe-annual-letter-2025-desktop.pdf); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/stripes-latest-data-startups-are-growing-50-faster-computer-demand-drove-50-of-gdp-growth-60-more-apps-yoy-and-more/) · `type: payment-processor data | geo: global/US | BR: med | tier: T1`. → *Implication:* "MVP ≤ 4 weeks" is achievable and now standard. It no longer differentiates a card; everyone can meet it, including competitors.
- **Supply surge:** ~600,000 new App Store apps in 2025 (+30%). 235,800 App Store submissions in Q1 2026 (+84% YoY), attributed to vibe coding. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/apple-app-store-experiences-surge-in-new-apps-amid-vibe-coding-boom-2000742653); [FindSkill.ai](https://findskill.ai/blog/vibe-coding-by-the-numbers/) · `type: news + aggregator (original likely Appfigures) | geo: global | BR: med | tier: T2/T3`. Shopify apps grew +71% from early 2024 to Q1 2026 ([StackDex](https://www.stackdex.io/research/state-of-shopify-apps-2026), T3). → *Implication:* assume any visible gap attracts copycats within months. Track M should score time-to-copy and require a non-code advantage (channel, data, local compliance knowledge, relationships).
- **Who is building:** "63% of vibe-coding users are non-developers" ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/vibe-coding-statistics/); [Taskade](https://www.taskade.com/blog/state-of-vibe-coding), T3). "34% of new micro-SaaS launched in Q1 2026 were built by founders with no prior coding experience" ([FindSkill.ai](https://findskill.ai/blog/vibe-coding-by-the-numbers/), T3, methodology unknown; treat as unverified). → *Implication:* coding skill is no longer a moat for the operator either. Domain and channel access become the scarce inputs.
- **Retention cost of AI-feature products:** AI-native GRR averages ~40%. At < $50/month, GRR is 23%; at $50–249, 45%; at > $250, 70% GRR and 85% NRR. "Easy to buy, easy to cancel"; the fix is habit, workflow fit, integrations, and clear differentiation from general LLMs. — [ChartMogul AI churn wave](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/); [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-ai-churn-wave) · `tier: T1 | BR: med`. → *Implication:* the harness should automatically flag "thin AI wrapper" cards (low ticket, no integration, replaceable by a general chatbot) as high churn risk.
- **"Wrapper" debate (opinion):** Stripe's CEO argued that calling AI startups "wrappers" misses the point, given their faster growth. — [TechCrunch, Feb 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/27/stripe-ceo-says-ai-startups-are-growing-faster-than-saas-ever-did-and-calling-them-wrappers-misses-the-point) · `tier: T4 opinion, based on the top-100 outlier cohort`. → *Implication:* growth among top AI companies is not evidence for the indie median. The ChartMogul retention data is the more relevant counterweight.
- **AI as a builder's tool rather than the product:** "68% of successful solo founders used AI primarily for development acceleration"; "AI coding assistants cut development time by ~50%". — [Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/) · `tier: T3/T4, unverified method`. → *Implication:* weak support for preferring cards where AI lowers the operator's cost (build, support, onboarding) over cards where AI *is* the value proposition.

### Inferences
- The AI era compresses the **build** moat to near zero and raises **supply**. The scarce assets for a one-person Brazil-first micro-SaaS are therefore (a) a reachable channel, (b) domain/regulatory knowledge (e.g., Brazilian fiscal and payment specifics like Pix Automático), and (c) a ticket high enough to escape the worst churn bands. These map directly onto the existing Track M criteria.
- Local complexity (Portuguese language, Brazilian regulation, Pix/boleto billing, WhatsApp-native workflows) plausibly acts as a moat against global vibe-coded competitors. That is a hypothesis, not data.
- AI raises support capacity for a solo operator, which loosens the "suporte baixo" constraint somewhat. No data quantifies this.

### Gaps
- No data on **price compression** in indie SaaS categories attributable to AI competition (2024–2026).
- No data on the **survival or churn of vibe-coded products**, or on how quickly copycats appear after a successful launch.
- No MicroConf/Freemius 2025–2026 cross-tabs on AI-product vs non-AI-product outcomes for indies.
- No Brazil-specific data on AI-era competition among local micro-SaaS.
