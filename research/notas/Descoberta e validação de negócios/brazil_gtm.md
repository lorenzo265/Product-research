## 1. Payments and recurring billing (Pix, Pix Automático, boleto, cards; Asaas, Iugu, Pagar.me, Stripe, Mercado Pago, Vindi)

### Takeaway
Pix Automático (recurring Pix) went live on 16 June 2025, and by May 2026 it was running about 2 million transactions a month. That is still tiny next to Pix as a whole, but it grows fast and brings in payers who had never held a digital subscription. Payers cannot be charged for it; the merchant's fee is whatever its payment provider sets. For a Brazilian-entity micro-SaaS, local gateways look cheaper than cards and cheaper than Stripe. Asaas charges about R$0.99–1.99 flat per Pix or boleto, while card fees run about 3.5–5% plus fixed fees. Stripe Pix is invite-only for Brazil-based businesses.

### Inferences
- Rough cost at R$30k MRR (inference from the fees above): if about 80% is collected by Pix/Pix Automático at a flat about R$1.99 on a R$150 average ticket (about 1.3%), with 20% on cards at about 4–5%, blended payment cost is about 2%, or about R$600/month. All-card billing would cost about 4.5–5%, or about R$1,400/month. The Pix-first mix saves about R$800/month, roughly 2.5–3% of MRR.
- Pix Automático volume (about 2M/month) is still small against total Pix (billions/month), so churn and retry behavior at scale is unproven. Keep card-on-file as a fallback and measure involuntary churn by rail.
- For the S track (few clients, high tickets), rail choice barely matters. Invoice by Pix charge or boleto plus NFS-e (Q2), and use a payment link for the first client.
- Stripe's role is the USD and international leg (see Q5), not BRL billing.

## 2. Legal and tax setup for a solo founder (MEI, ME/Simples, Anexo III vs V and Fator R, NFS-e 2026, Reforma Tributária, LGPD)

### Takeaway
MEI is not a legal vehicle for software development or IT services, and its R$81k/year ceiling is far below both tracks' targets anyway. The practical setup is an ME, typically an SLU (single-member limited company), in Simples Nacional under software CNAEs. There, the Fator R (payroll including pró-labore ≥ 28% of revenue) moves the company from Anexo V (from 15.5%) to Anexo III (from 6%). Two 2026 items are time-critical. The national NFS-e standard is now mandatory. And the 1–30 September 2026 window (Resolução CGSN 186/2026) decides whether a Simples company pays IBS/CBS inside the DAS or under the regular regime for H1 2027. LGPD's small-agent regime lightens the compliance load (no mandatory DPO) but does not remove it.

### Cited Findings
- Software development and programming (for example CNAE 6201-5/01 and 6202-3/00) are not permitted MEI occupations in 2026. - The MEI ceiling stays R$81,000/year in 2026, unchanged since 2018. PLP 108/2021 passed the Senate 71–0 with R$130k. ### Inferences
- The tax gap between annexes is large for a solo founder. At R$30k MRR, gross revenue over 12 months is about R$360k. In Anexo V, even at the 15.5% starting rate, tax would be at least about R$56k/yr; the effective rate at this revenue band is likely higher (bracket tables not retrieved). In Anexo III (about 6% start, rising with the bracket), it is well under half that. Paying pró-labore ≥ 28% of revenue has its own costs: the employee INSS contribution and IRPF on pró-labore. This is the standard trade-off accountants model, but neither INSS rates nor the bracket effective rates were verified in this session.
- The September 2026 IBS/CBS "por fora" choice matters mostly if customers are larger companies under the regular regime that care about credits. For SMB customers that are themselves in Simples or MEI, staying inside the DAS is probably simpler. Inference only; confirm with an accountant.
- Opening an SLU plus a monthly accountant fee (amount not researched) is the day-1 legal cost for both tracks. The S track can invoice its first client as soon as the CNPJ, municipal registration and NFS-e access exist.

## 5. Geographic arbitrage (selling abroad in USD from Brazil; bringing proven foreign products to Brazil)

### Takeaway
Only payment-side signals were verified. Cross-border processors already offer Pix and recurring Pix to foreign merchants: EBANX claims 38% of Pix Automático volume and recurring methods in 12 emerging markets. Stripe offers Pix to users outside Brazil but keeps it invite-only for Brazil-based businesses, and charges about +2% for international cards. So "we accept Pix" is no longer a durable moat against foreign entrants. Moats that remain plausible but were not verified this session: NFS-e and Simples/IBS-CBS compliance, WhatsApp-native workflows, and Portuguese support. Export-of-services tax treatment and FX mechanics for selling in USD were not verified.

### Cited Findings
- EBANX processes a reported 38% of Pix Automático transactions in Brazil and has extended recurring alternative payment methods to 12 emerging markets, "opening a potential user base of over 1 billion users for global merchants". — [CartaCapital](https://www.cartacapital.com.br/do-micro-ao-macro/pix-automatico-novos-assinantes-economia-digital-ebanx/) · [PRESS]/[COMPANY-DATA] · Implication M (foreign-to-Brazil): foreign SaaS can bill Brazilians in BRL via recurring Pix without a local entity. A localized clone must win on compliance, WhatsApp and language, not on payment method alone.
- Stripe: Pix is invite-only for Brazil-based businesses. — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-enable-pix-as-a-payment-method-in-brazil) · [VENDOR]. A 2025 LinkedIn post announced that Stripe users can accept Pix in Brazil, apparently for non-Brazilian accounts; details not verified. — [LinkedIn (Marcel van Oost)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/marcelvanoost_%F0%9D%99%85%F0%9D%99%90%F0%9D%99%8E%F0%9D%99%8F-%F0%9D%99%84%F0%9D%99%89-stripe-users-can-now-accept-activity-7360682506766946305-XYRF) · [LOWER-TIER] · Implication M: a founder selling to both Brazil and abroad might run two stacks, a local gateway for BRL and Stripe (or a foreign entity) for USD.
